Biomass (ESA)

ESA's Biomass satellite launched on April 29, 2025 on Vega-C.

Image: NASA.

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • What is it? The first P-band A radar on a moving satellite that combines many echoes into one sharp picture, as if it had a giant antenna. Glossary in space.
  • Why does it matter? To measure the woody The total amount of living material, like the wood and leaves in a forest. Measuring it shows how much carbon plants store. Glossary where forests store carbon, and track deforestation and regrowth.

The Five W's of Biomass

Who

ESA (European Space Agency).

Source: ESA, Biomass launched

What

The first P-band synthetic aperture A tool that sends out radio waves and listens for the echoes. From space it can see the ground through clouds, day or night. Glossary in space.

Source: ESA, Biomass launched

When

Launched April 29, 2025 on Vega-C.

Source: ESA, Biomass launched

Where

Earth The curved path an object takes as it travels around a planet, moon or star, like a satellite circling Earth. Glossary, looking through the world's forest canopies.

Source: ESA, Biomass launched

Why

To measure the woody biomass where forests store carbon, and track deforestation and regrowth.

Source: ESA, Biomass launched

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

ESA's Biomass satellite launched on April 29, 2025 on Vega-C. It carries the first P-band synthetic aperture radar in space; its long wavelength (about 70 cm) reaches through forest canopies to measure the woody trunks and branches where carbon is stored.

Source: ESA, Biomass launched to count forest carbon

Its maps of forest carbon, height and disturbance give the world a better count of deforestation and regrowth. See our Forests hub for the groups replanting on the ground.
