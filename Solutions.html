Earth Observation

Image: Syced, via Wikimedia Commons (CC0)

ALOS-4 "DAICHI-4" (JAXA)

JAXA's DAICHI-4 radar sees through clouds over a 200 km swath, continuing the radar record used for deforestation alerts.

Image: NASA (ISS074-E-0492148)

Biomass (ESA)

ESA's Biomass satellite, launched April 29, 2025, carries the first P-band radar in space, seeing through forest canopies to measure the carbon stored in trees.

Image: NASA Earth Observatory/Lauren Dauphin, Landsat data from USGS (public domain)

CHIME Hyperspectral Imaging Mission (ESA)

CHIME will image Earth in more than 200 colours of light to support sustainable farming, food security and biodiversity (ESA).

Image: NASA Earth Observatory

Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS)

Europe's air monitoring service forecasts air pollution, smoke and dust worldwide twice a day for more than 23,500 users, free and open.

Image: ESA/Mlabspace (CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO)

Copernicus CO2M Carbon Dioxide Monitoring (ESA / EU)

A planned trio of satellites that will measure the carbon dioxide and methane people release, so countries can check whether their emission cuts are working.

Image: NASA (ISS073-E-0420617)

Copernicus Emergency Management Service: On-Demand Mapping

The European Union's emergency mapping service turns satellite images into maps for disaster teams, 24/7 since 2012, with more than 1,000 activations.

Image: NASA-JPL/Caltech/ESA/Copernicus (PIA21962)

Copernicus Sentinel-1 (ESA)

Sentinel-1 radar sees through clouds, day and night, to map floods, earthquakes and ice; Sentinel-1C and 1D joined in 2024 and 2025.

Image: contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2024), processed by ESA (CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO)

Copernicus Sentinel-2C (ESA)

Sentinel-2C joined the Copernicus fleet in September 2024, imaging land and coasts in 13 bands so forests and crops are checked every five days.

Image: contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2018), processed by EUMETSAT (CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO)

Copernicus Sentinel-3 (ESA / EUMETSAT)

The Copernicus Sentinel-3 satellites measure Earth's oceans, land, ice and atmosphere, and a third satellite, Sentinel-3C, launched on September 15, 2026 (ESA).

Image: contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2025), processed by ESA (CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO)

Copernicus Sentinel-4 (ESA / EUMETSAT)

From geostationary orbit on MTG-S1, Sentinel-4 measures air pollution such as nitrogen dioxide and ozone over Europe and North Africa every hour (ESA).

Image: NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center (public domain)

Copernicus Sentinel-5P (ESA)

Copernicus Sentinel-5P maps the gases in the air we breathe, covering the whole planet every day with its Tropomi instrument (ESA).

Image: NASA/Carla Thomas (AFRC2025-0170-03)

Copernicus Sentinel-6B (ESA / NASA)

Sentinel-6B, launched November 17, 2025, continues the precise record of global sea level that began in the early 1990s.

Image: NASA (iss073e0604445)

Digital Earth Africa

Digital Earth Africa turns satellite data into free and open maps of water, farmland and coasts for the whole African continent (Digital Earth Africa).

Image: NASA/NOAA DSCOVR EPIC

DSCOVR / EPIC (NASA–NOAA)

A camera photographing the entire sunlit face of Earth several times a day.

Image: NASA/Jeff Williams (ISS020-E-047807)

EarthCARE (ESA–JAXA)

ESA–JAXA EarthCARE carries the first space cloud radar that can measure how cloud particles move up and down, sharpening forecasts of how clouds affect climate.

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA25593)

EMIT (NASA)

NASA's EMIT instrument on the International Space Station maps the minerals in desert dust and has spotted methane super-emitters from space (NASA JPL).

Image: ISRO/NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA14742)

EOS-06 / Oceansat-3 (ISRO)

India's EOS-06, the third Oceansat, tracks ocean color, sea-surface temperature and winds; it imaged Cyclone Mandous and an algal bloom on day one.

Image: NASA

FireSat (Earth Fire Alliance)

Earth Fire Alliance's FireSat satellites are built to spot wildfires as small as 5 by 5 meters from space and get the data to firefighters fast.

Image: NASA (ISS073-E-0420604)

FIRMS Fire Information for Resource Management System (NASA)

NASA's FIRMS delivers satellite fire detections within 3 hours anywhere on Earth, and in real time for the US and Canada. Free for firefighters and everyone.

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (public domain)

FLEX and Sentinel-3C (ESA)

Launched together on September 15, 2026, ESA's FLEX maps the faint glow of photosynthesis while Sentinel-3C watches oceans and land.

Image: NOAA NESDIS (public domain)

FORUM Far-Infrared Mission (ESA)

ESA's FORUM will measure Earth's outgoing far-infrared radiation, never measured from space before, filling a missing piece of the climate jigsaw (ESA).

Image: ESA-Anneke Le Floc'h (CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO)

Galileo Satellite Navigation (EU / ESA)

Europe's Galileo is the world's most precise satellite navigation system, serving over five billion smartphone users and helping rescues (ESA).

Image: NASA (ISS074-E-0046371)

GCOM-C "SHIKISAI" (JAXA)

Japan's SHIKISAI satellite checks the health of Earth from space every 2 or 3 days, tracking clouds, aerosols, ocean color, plants, snow and ice (JAXA).

Image: NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio (public domain)

GeoXO Geostationary Extended Observations (NOAA / NASA)

NOAA's next generation of geostationary weather satellites, planned from 2032, will improve warnings of severe weather and hazards (NOAA).

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (public domain)

GOSAT-GW "IBUKI GW" (JAXA)

JAXA's IBUKI GW tracks carbon dioxide, methane and nitrogen dioxide while also measuring the water cycle. Launched June 29, 2025 from Tanegashima Space Center.

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA22431)

GRACE-FO (NASA / Germany)

Twin US-German satellites weigh Earth's water by measuring gravity, tracking groundwater, ice and sea level since 2018. Active.

Image: NASA/Goddard

GSMaP Global Rainfall Map (JAXA)

JAXA's Global Satellite Mapping of Precipitation maps rain around the world every hour, used by 18,673 registered users in 163 countries and regions.

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech/GSFC (PIA19877) (public domain)

HydroGNSS (ESA)

ESA's first Scout mission uses reflected navigation-satellite signals to measure soil moisture, wetlands and frozen ground (ESA).

Image: NASA (STS102-357-031)

INSAT-3DS Weather Satellite (ISRO)

India's INSAT-3DS watches weather, land and oceans from geostationary orbit and carries a search-and-rescue transponder (ISRO).

Image: NASA Earth Observatory

International Charter: Space and Major Disasters

When disaster strikes, 17 space agencies and satellite operators pool their satellites and give free images to rescue teams. Activated 1,000 times.

Image: NASA/Liz Wilk (KSC-20221109-PH-LNW01_0001)

Joint Polar Satellite System (NOAA)

NOAA's polar weather satellites circle Earth 14 times a day. Polar-orbiting satellites supply about 85% of the data used by weather prediction models.

Image: NASA/Kim Shiflett (KSC-20210927-PH-KLS04_0013)

Landsat 9 (NASA / USGS)

Landsat 9 continues a 50-year record of Earth’s land, collecting 740 scenes a day and, with Landsat 8, seeing each place every 8 days.

Image: NASA Goddard Space Flight Center (public domain)

Libera Earth Energy Instrument (NASA)

Libera will measure how much energy leaves Earth each day, keeping a climate record that began in 2000 going strong (NOAA).

Image: NASA (ISS073-E-0080625)

Meteosat Third Generation (ESA / EUMETSAT)

Three weather satellites now watch Europe and Africa from 36,000 km up, with new images every 2.5 minutes and a camera that catches lightning day and night.

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA26113)

Methane Alert and Response System (UNEP)

The UN's satellite methane alert system has sent more than 3,500 alerts about major leaks in 33 countries, and responses rose from 1% to 12% in a year.

Image: ESA/ATG medialab (CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO)

MetOp-SG-A1 and Sentinel-5A (ESA / EUMETSAT)

Launched August 2025 on Ariane 6, MetOp-SG-A1 carries Sentinel-5, which delivers daily global data on air pollutants, ozone and climate gases.

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA26285, illustration)

NISAR (NASA–ISRO)

NISAR, a NASA and ISRO radar satellite launched from India on July 30, 2025, scans nearly all of Earth's land and ice twice every 12 days, even through clouds.

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA18935)

OCO-2 Orbiting Carbon Observatory (NASA)

NASA's OCO-2 satellite has measured carbon dioxide around the planet since 2014, showing where carbon is released and where forests and oceans absorb it.

Image: NASA/Kim Shiflett (KSC-20231204-PH-KLS01_0024)

PACE (NASA)

NASA's PACE reads the color of the ocean to track phytoplankton, the base of the marine food web, plus aerosols and clouds.

Image: NASA (ISS070-E-086805)

RADARSAT Constellation Mission (Canada)

Three Canadian radar satellites watch Canada's land, coasts and Arctic day and night, through cloud and smoke, with about 250,000 images a year.

Also in: Polar

Image: NOAA (2268-532) (public domain)

SARSAT Search and Rescue Satellites (NOAA)

NOAA's SARSAT satellites relay distress beacon signals to rescuers; the international system has supported more than 50,000 rescues worldwide since 1982 (NOAA).

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA19133, illustration)

SMAP Soil Moisture Active Passive (NASA)

SMAP maps the water in Earth's soil from space, helping improve predictions of weather, floods and droughts (NASA).

Image: CNES (PIA25623)

SWOT (NASA–CNES)

SWOT measures more than 95% of Earth's lakes larger than 15 acres and rivers wider than 330 feet, the first global survey of fresh water.

Image: Carbon Mapper/Planet Labs PBC (PIA26416)

Tanager-1 (Carbon Mapper / Planet)

Tanager-1, built by Planet with a NASA JPL-designed spectrometer for the nonprofit Carbon Mapper, pinpoints methane and CO2 plumes from single sources.

Image: NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio (public domain)

TEMPO Air Quality Mission (NASA)

From geostationary orbit, NASA's TEMPO measures air pollution over North America every daylight hour, a first from space (NASA).

The GLOBE Program

A worldwide science program where students and citizen scientists collect real environmental data: more than 293 million measurements from 127 countries.

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA26652)

The Science of Tsunami Detection

Deep-ocean buoys, satellites, GPS signals and seafloor cables spot tsunamis in the open ocean so coastal communities get time to reach high ground (NOAA, NASA).

Also in: Ocean

Forests

Image: NASA (iss071e047913)

African Parks

African Parks manages 24 protected areas in 13 countries, covering over 20 million hectares, in partnership with governments and local communities (nonprofit).

Image: Intermountain Region US Forest Service (public domain)

American Forests

Since 1875, American Forests has worked to restore U.S. forests, from big natural landscapes to city streets, with more than 63 million trees planted.

Image: Gary Peeples/USFWS (public domain)

Arbor Day Foundation

Since 1972, the Arbor Day Foundation says it has planted more than half a billion trees, working in more than 60 countries (nonprofit figures).

Image: Heiner Amado Cadillo via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)

Cool Earth Basic Income Pilot (Peruvian Amazon)

Cool Earth gave 211 Indigenous people in three communities in Peru's central Amazon an unconditional basic income for two years (University of Bath).

Image: NASA (iss062e001866)

Costa Rica Payments for Environmental Services (FONAFIFO)

Costa Rica's FONAFIFO pays the owners of forests and forest plantations for the environmental services they provide, under Forestry Law 7575 (FONAFIFO).

Eden: People+Planet

Eden: People+Planet restores landscapes with local communities, including a 394,000-hectare project in Tanzania's Rubeho Mountains (Eden).

Image: NASA (ISS056-E-127068)

European Forest Fire Information System (EFFIS)

Europe's wildfire system forecasts fire danger and maps burnt areas from satellites. In 2024 it mapped wildfires in 42 countries.

Image: NASA (ISS007-E-06305)

Forest Elephants, Carbon Gardeners

By thinning small trees, forest elephants help central Africa's rainforests store more carbon: 26 to 60 more tonnes of biomass per hectare (Nature Geoscience).

Image: NASA (iss068e025397)

Galápagos Giant Tortoise Rewilding

Galápagos Conservancy and the Galápagos National Park Directorate rear giant tortoises in breeding centres and return them to their home islands.

Image: NASA (iss059e088632)

GEDI Forest Lidar (NASA)

GEDI fires lasers from the International Space Station to measure the height and 3D shape of Earth's forests (University of Maryland).

Image: NASA (STS046-103-053)

Global Forest Watch (WRI)

Free satellite forest data and deforestation alerts from WRI; where Indigenous forest monitors used it, deforestation fell 52% in one year.

Image: Judy Gallagher via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)

Gorongosa National Park Restoration

The Mozambique government and the Gorongosa Project are restoring Gorongosa National Park as "a park for the people" (Gorongosa).

Image: NASA (ISS012-E-11654)

Great Green Wall (African Union / UNCCD)

An African-led effort to restore land across the Sahel. A 2020 UN status report counted about 4 million hectares restored inside the Great Green Wall zone.

Image: NASA (STS072-722-004)

Justdiggit

With farmers and pastoralists in Africa, Justdiggit is restoring 525,000 hectares of dry land and has brought back 32.3 million trees (organization figures).

Image: NZ Department of Conservation via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)

Kākāpō Recovery (New Zealand)

New Zealand's Department of Conservation and Ngāi Tahu have rebuilt the kākāpō from just 51 birds in 1995 (NZ DOC).

Image: SI Partner

Life Terra

Life Terra and its partners have planted more than 36 million trees across Europe (company figure), working toward a goal of 500 million trees.

Image: NASA/Jessica Meir (iss074e0492148)

MAAP, Amazon Deforestation Watch

MAAP, an initiative of the nonprofit Amazon Conservation, uses satellite images to report on urgent deforestation and fires in the Amazon.

Image: NASA/Glenn Benson (KSC-20230412-PH-GEB01_0006)

Mangrove Forests

About 80 species of mangrove trees guard tropical coastlines: their tangled roots hold mud in place, soften storm surges and give fish food and shelter.

Image: Diego Delso via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Mountain Gorilla Recovery

Rangers, vets and communities in Uganda, Rwanda and the DR Congo pulled mountain gorillas up from Critically Endangered (IUCN).

Image: Ambish Kazi via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Nepal Doubles Its Tigers

With rangers, wildlife corridors and over 450 community anti-poaching units, Nepal nearly tripled its wild tigers in 13 years (NTNC).

Image: USDA Forest Service Intermountain Region (public domain)

One Tree Planted

Since 2014, One Tree Planted has planted more than 171 million trees with 489 partners in 84 countries (organization figure).

Image: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Southeast Region (public domain)

Plant-for-the-Planet

Started by a 9-year-old in 2007, Plant-for-the-Planet trains young Climate Justice Ambassadors and restores forests, including Mexico's Mayan forests.

Image: NASA (iss071e073568)

Predator Free 2050 (New Zealand)

Predator Free 2050 is a nationwide goal to eradicate rats, possums, feral cats and mustelids from Aotearoa New Zealand, led by the Department of Conservation.

Image: NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center/Landsat (public domain)

Rainforest Connection

Solar-powered Guardian listening devices and SI detect chainsaws in real time; Rainforest Connection reports more than 726,000 hectares of rainforest protected.

Image: Addshore via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Redonda Island Restoration

Redonda went from a dusty moonscape to a wildlife haven and is now one of the largest protected areas in the Caribbean (Fauna & Flora).

Image: NASA/Goddard

Rewilding Europe

Rewilding Europe helps wild nature come back across Europe. Rewilding Europe, working with local partners in large pioneering landscapes.

Image: Gonzalo Lastra via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Route of Parks of Patagonia (Chile)

In January 2018, Chile and Tompkins Conservation signed the decrees creating a network of new national parks across Patagonia (Tompkins Conservation).

Image: NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center

Saharan Dust Feeds the Amazon

Every year about 27.7 million tons of Saharan dust falls on the Amazon, delivering some 22,000 tons of phosphorus that the rainforest needs (NASA).

Image: Yakov Fedorov via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Saiga Recovery on the Kazakh Steppe (Altyn Dala)

The Altyn Dala Conservation Initiative, led by ACBK, is restoring Kazakhstan's steppe, with the saiga antelope as its flagship (Altyn Dala).

Image: SI Partner

Terraformation

Terraformation grows native, biodiverse forests from seed to canopy across the tropics and Hawaii, funding seed collection, nurseries, planting and monitoring.

Image: NASA (ISS013-E-74843)

The Amazon Makes Its Own Rainy Season

Water breathed out by Amazon trees starts the rainy season two to three months before winds alone would bring it (NASA JPL).

Image: Charles J. Sharp via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)

The Arabian Oryx Comeback

Captive breeding and reintroductions brought the Arabian oryx back, with about 1,000 in the wild by 2011 (IUCN).

Image: Diego Delso via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

The Iberian Lynx Comeback

Spain and Portugal brought the Iberian lynx back from the edge: the 2025 census counted 2,663, almost double 2021 (MITECO).

Image: SI Partner

Three Trees for Every One Removed

Saving Planets' own forest rule: plant three trees for every one removed, so each removal becomes a net gain for habitat, soil and shade, and is easy to verify.

Image: Chris via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)

Trees for Life: Restoring the Caledonian Forest

Trees for Life is rewilding the Scottish Highlands, bringing back the Caledonian forest, of which less than 2% survives (Trees for Life).

Image: contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2020), processed by ESA (CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO)

Trees for the Future

Partnering with East African farmers on regenerative farms: 450M+ trees grown and 99,000 acres restored (nonprofit figures).

Image: USDA Forest Service (public domain)

WeForest

WeForest has grown more than 118 million trees and has 100,000 hectares of forest under restoration and conservation (WeForest).

Image: NASA (ISS023-E-007925)

Yellowstone Wolves

Gray wolves returned to Yellowstone in 1995 after nearly 70 years; in January 2026 at least 84 lived in the park, in eight packs (NPS).

Lakes

Image: NASA/Goddard

Adopt-a-Beach (Alliance for the Great Lakes)

Each year, Adopt-a-Beach volunteers remove tens of thousands of pounds of litter from Great Lakes beaches, about 85% of it plastic.

Image: Mohammad hajeer via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Azraq Wetland Reserve Restoration (Jordan)

Since 1994, Jordan's Royal Society for the Conservation of Nature has been restoring the Azraq wetland in the eastern desert (RSCN).

Image: Phadke09 via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Chilika Lake Restoration

In 2000, the Chilika Development Authority opened a new mouth between India's Chilika Lake and the sea, and the lagoon and its fisheries recovered (CDA).

Clean Lakes Alliance (Madison, Wisconsin)

Clean Lakes Alliance works to improve water quality in Madison's Yahara lakes; it reports 81 monitoring stations and over $1.4 million in grants (its figures).

Image: NASA (iss064e015104)

Clean Up the Lake

Clean Up the Lake removes submerged litter from freshwater lakes using scuba divers and volunteers, and GPS-pins, sorts and weighs every piece it finds.

Image: NASA (ISS074-E-0642367)

Great Lakes Plastic Cleanup

The Great Lakes Plastic Cleanup captures plastic across the world's largest freshwater system using Seabins, drones, and beach robots.

Image: NASA (ISS071-E-14284)

Keep Tahoe Blue (League to Save Lake Tahoe)

Keep Tahoe Blue, the League to Save Lake Tahoe, has protected the lake for decades, and its volunteers picked up 1,330 pounds of litter after July 4, 2026.

Image: NASA (ISS007-E-17620)

Lake Biwa and the Soap Movement

After a red tide in 1977, Shiga residents switched to soap, and in 1980 the prefecture limited phosphorus to protect Lake Biwa (Shiga Prefecture).

Image: NASA (ISS030-E-161620)

Lake Constance Phosphorus Recovery

Treatment plants that remove phosphorus brought Lake Constance back to the clear state typical of Alpine lakes (International Commission for the Protection of Lake Constance).

Image: NOAA Great Lakes Environmental Research Laboratory (public domain)

Lake Erie Harmful Algal Bloom Forecast (NOAA)

NOAA forecasts and tracks toxic algae in western Lake Erie through the summer so cities and swimmers can keep drinking water and people safe (NOAA NCCOS).

Image: NASA/Bill White (KSC-20170412-PH_SWW01_0081)

Lake Plastic Recovery

Saving Planets' own lake solution: recover plastic from freshwater lakes by skimming, shoreline collection and divers, before it travels downstream to the sea.

Living Lakes (Global Nature Fund)

Since 1998, the Global Nature Fund's Living Lakes Network has protected and restored lakes and wetlands with 130 member organizations in more than 60 countries.

Restoring the Lake Depths Foundation

A Nevada nonprofit whose ROVs and volunteer divers pull submerged trash from Lake Tahoe, from the surface down to 1,640 feet: over 13 tons in summer 2024 alone.

Image: Christophe95 via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Tonle Sap: The Lake That Reverses a River

Tonle Sap, the largest freshwater lake in Southeast Asia, is a UNESCO biosphere reserve whose flooded forests shelter villages and fish (UNESCO).

Ocean

Created by SI and Biological SI Partner

4ocean

4ocean removes plastic from oceans, rivers, and coastlines with full-time cleanup crews, funded by products made from recovered plastic.

Image: NASA (iss071e378611)

Abu Dhabi Mangrove Restoration

The Environment Agency – Abu Dhabi tracks more than 18.5 million mangroves planted across over 1,775 hectares with its new Mangrove Observatory.

Image: NASA (GSFC_20171208_Archive_e001460)

Alaska Kelp and Seaweed Farming

More than 20 kelp farms grow ribbon, giant, bull and sugar kelp in Alaska's cold, nutrient-rich waters (Alaska Mariculture Alliance).

Image: NASA (iss072e820937)

Alaska SeaLife Center: Marine Wildlife Rescue

The Alaska SeaLife Center in Seward is a nonprofit aquarium and research center that rescues stranded marine animals and studies Alaska's marine ecosystems.

Image: Ryan Hodnett via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Andfjorden Marine Protected Area (Norway)

On 18 September 2026, Norway's government created the Andfjorden Marine Protected Area across Andøy, Harstad and Senja (Norwegian government).

Image: NOAA

Argo Ocean Float Array

Close to 4,000 robotic floats dive through the ocean and send back temperature and salinity data, free to everyone. The 3 millionth profile came in 2024.

Image: Adam U / NOAA/NMFS/Pacific Islands Fisheries Science Center (public domain)

Argos Satellite Tracking System

A satellite system that tracks wildlife and ocean instruments from tiny transmitters, following more than 8,000 animals every month for conservation science.

Image: NASA Johnson Space Center, ISS crew photo ISS018-E-024351

Atoll Rewilding: Seabirds Against Sea-Level Rise

A 2026 study of 28 atoll islands found that removing rats and restoring native plants boosts the seabirds, reefs and sand that help atolls keep up with rising seas.

Image: NASA (STS039-88-054)

Billion Oyster Project

A New York nonprofit restoring the harbor with oyster reefs, aiming for one billion oysters by 2030 (nonprofit figures).

Image: NASA Earth Observatory image by Joshua Stevens, using MODIS data from LANCE/EOSDIS Rapid Response

Bristol Bay Salmon Habitat Protection (EPA)

In January 2023, the U.S. EPA used Clean Water Act section 404(c) to limit mine waste disposal in Bristol Bay headwaters near the Pebble deposit.

Image: NASA/GSFC/Jeff Schmaltz/MODIS Land Rapid Response Team

Copernicus Marine Service

Free, open ocean data and forecasts for the whole planet. In 2025 users downloaded almost 15,000 terabytes of ocean data from Copernicus Marine.

Image: NASA (STS093-717-066)

Coral Gardeners

Coral Gardeners grows corals in nurseries and replants them on reefs in French Polynesia, Fiji and Thailand: 238,250 corals planted so far.

Image: G.P. Schmahl/NOAA (public domain)

Coral IVF (Great Barrier Reef Foundation)

Researchers capture coral spawn and rear millions of baby corals for damaged reefs; the first Coral IVF corals reproduced on the Great Barrier Reef in 2021.

Image: NASA (ISS068-E-004262)

Coral Reef Watch (NOAA)

NOAA's Coral Reef Watch uses satellites to warn of the ocean heat that bleaches corals, with daily monitoring of 95% of the world's coral reefs.

Image: NASA/GSFC/METI/ERSDAC/JAROS and U.S./Japan ASTER Science Team (PIA03890)

Coral Restoration Foundation

Coral Restoration Foundation had returned 253,833 corals to Florida's reefs by the end of 2024 (organization figure). Florida's Coral Reef in the Florida Keys.

Image: NASA/JPL/UCSD/JSC (PIA11019)

Coral Vita Land-Based Coral Farms

Coral Vita grows reef-building corals on land up to 50 times faster than in nature, and says it has grown over 100,000 corals (company figures).

Image: Alaska Fisheries Science Center, Marine Observer Program (public domain)

Global Fishing Watch

Global Fishing Watch uses satellites and SI to map fishing; its 2024 Nature study found about 75% of industrial fishing vessels are not publicly tracked.

Image: NASA/Goddard/NPP (GSFC_20171208_Archive_e000038)

Gulf of Alaska Keeper: Marine Debris Cleanup

Gulf of Alaska Keeper (GoAK) is a nonprofit that removes marine debris from the remote shores of Prince William Sound and the northern Gulf of Alaska (GoAK).

Heal the Bay

Since 1985, Heal the Bay has worked to make the coastal waters and watersheds of Greater Los Angeles safe, healthy and clean (Heal the Bay).

Image: NOAA (public domain)

Healthy Seas

Volunteer divers recover ghost nets: Healthy Seas reports 1,309.1 tons of nets and marine litter removed since 2013 (Healthy Seas figure).

Image: Karlyn Langjahr/NOAA (public domain)

International Coastal Cleanup (Ocean Conservancy)

Since 1986, more than 19 million volunteers have collected more than 400 million pounds of trash from beaches and waterways (Ocean Conservancy).

Image: NASA (ISS018-E-018110)

Kelp Forests

Kelp can grow up to 18 inches a day, building underwater forests that feed and shelter thousands of species. Nature, studied and protected by NOAA and partners.

Image: NASA (ISS040-E-018752)

Mesoamerican Reef Insurance Programme (MAR Fund)

The MAR Insurance Programme, run by the nonprofit MAR Fund, uses parametric insurance to pay for fast reef repair after hurricanes along the 1,000 km Mesoamerican Reef.

Image: NASA (ISS071-E-449837)

Mission Blue Hope Spots
