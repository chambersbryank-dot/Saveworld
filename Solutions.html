Earth Observation (Top)

Photo: Syced, via Wikimedia Commons (CC0). A 1/50 scale model of ALOS-4 at JAXA's Tsukuba Space Center.

ALOS-4 "DAICHI-4" (JAXA)

JAXA's DAICHI-4 radar sees through clouds over a 200 km swath, continuing the radar record used for deforestation alerts. A radar satellite with the PALSAR-3 L-band synthetic aperture radar and a ship-tracking receiver.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

Biomass (ESA)

ESA's Biomass satellite, launched April 29, 2025, carries the first P-band radar in space, seeing through forest canopies to measure the carbon stored in trees. The first P-band synthetic aperture radar in space.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA Earth Observatory (Aqua MODIS, June 19, 2013).

Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS)

Europe's air monitoring service forecasts air pollution, smoke and dust worldwide twice a day for more than 23,500 users, free and open. Quality-assured information about the atmosphere, especially trace gases and aerosols, from satellites, ground stations and models.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

Copernicus CO2M Carbon Dioxide Monitoring (ESA / EU)

A planned trio of satellites that will measure the carbon dioxide and methane people release, so countries can check whether their emission cuts are working. Each satellite carries a near-infrared and shortwave-infrared spectrometer that measures carbon dioxide and methane in the air, focusing on emissions from human activity. It also measures nitrogen dioxide and tiny airborne particles.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS073-E-0420617, Aug. 3, 2025).

Copernicus Emergency Management Service: On-Demand Mapping

The European Union's emergency mapping service turns satellite images into maps for disaster teams, 24/7 since 2012, with more than 1,000 activations. Rapid Mapping right after a disaster, plus Risk and Recovery Mapping for preparing and rebuilding, all made from satellite images.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (contains Copernicus Sentinel-1 data, ESA).

Copernicus Sentinel-1 (ESA)

Sentinel-1 radar sees through clouds, day and night, to map floods, earthquakes and ice; Sentinel-1C and 1D joined in 2024 and 2025. Radar satellites imaging Earth through cloud and darkness.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

Copernicus Sentinel-2C (ESA)

Sentinel-2C joined the Copernicus fleet in September 2024, imaging land and coasts in 13 bands so forests and crops are checked every five days. A satellite with a multispectral imager covering 13 spectral bands, with a 290 km swath and detail down to 10 m.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA, International Space Station.

Copernicus Sentinel-3 (ESA / EUMETSAT)

The Copernicus Sentinel-3 satellites measure Earth's oceans, land, ice and atmosphere, and a third satellite, Sentinel-3C, launched on September 15, 2026 (ESA). Satellites with instruments for ocean and land color (21 bands), sea and land temperature with two dedicated fire channels, sea height and more.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (iss071e207494).

Copernicus Sentinel-4 (ESA / EUMETSAT)

From geostationary orbit on MTG-S1, Sentinel-4 measures air pollution such as nitrogen dioxide and ozone over Europe and North Africa every hour (ESA). An ultraviolet, visible and near-infrared spectrometer that measures pollutants such as nitrogen dioxide and ozone.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

Copernicus Sentinel-5P (ESA)

Copernicus Sentinel-5P maps the gases in the air we breathe, covering the whole planet every day with its Tropomi instrument (ESA). The first Copernicus mission dedicated to monitoring our atmosphere. Its Tropomi instrument maps trace gases, aerosols and clouds.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

Copernicus Sentinel-6B (ESA / NASA)

Sentinel-6B, launched November 17, 2025, continues the precise record of global sea level that began in the early 1990s. A radar altimetry satellite that measures the height of the sea surface.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS070-E-086805, Feb. 3, 2024).

CryoSat Ice Mission (ESA)

CryoSat measures polar sea ice and the great ice sheets from space, and its data revealed 85 hidden lakes under Antarctica (ESA). A satellite carrying a radar altimeter built for ice. It measures the thickness of polar sea ice and changes in the ice sheets of Greenland and Antarctica.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/NOAA DSCOVR EPIC.

DSCOVR / EPIC (NASA–NOAA)

A camera photographing the entire sunlit face of Earth several times a day.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

EarthCARE (ESA–JAXA)

ESA–JAXA EarthCARE carries the first space cloud radar that can measure how cloud particles move up and down, sharpening forecasts of how clouds affect climate. A satellite with four instruments, including JAXA's Doppler Cloud Profiling Radar.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech.

EMIT (NASA)

NASA's EMIT instrument on the International Space Station maps the minerals in desert dust and has spotted methane super-emitters from space (NASA JPL). EMIT is an imaging spectrometer that measures visible and infrared light. It takes more than 100,000 spectra every second across a 72-kilometer (39-mile) field of view.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech/ISRO.

EOS-06 / Oceansat-3 (ISRO)

India's EOS-06, the third Oceansat, tracks ocean color, sea-surface temperature and winds; it imaged Cyclone Mandous and an algal bloom on day one. An ocean satellite with color, temperature and wind sensors.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (MODIS, Terra satellite).

FireSat (Earth Fire Alliance)

Earth Fire Alliance's FireSat satellites are built to spot wildfires as small as 5 by 5 meters from space and get the data to firefighters fast. FireSat is the first satellite constellation designed specifically for wildfires. Its infrared instruments can detect fires as small as 5 by 5 meters, about the size of two parking spaces, and measure how intense a fire is.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS073-E-0420604, Aug. 3, 2025).

FIRMS Fire Information for Resource Management System (NASA)

NASA's FIRMS delivers satellite fire detections within 3 hours anywhere on Earth, and in real time for the US and Canada. Free for firefighters and everyone. Active fire and hotspot data from the MODIS instruments on Aqua and Terra and the VIIRS instruments on Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech.

FLEX and Sentinel-3C (ESA)

Launched together on September 15, 2026, ESA's FLEX maps the faint glow of photosynthesis while Sentinel-3C watches oceans and land. FLEX maps plant fluorescence; Sentinel-3C measures ocean and land.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS074-E-0046371, Jan. 3, 2026).

GCOM-C "SHIKISAI" (JAXA)

Japan's SHIKISAI satellite checks the health of Earth from space every 2 or 3 days, tracking clouds, aerosols, ocean color, plants, snow and ice (JAXA). A satellite carrying SGLI, an optical sensor that sees from near-ultraviolet to thermal infrared light at 250 m to 1 km detail.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech.

GOSAT-GW "IBUKI GW" (JAXA)

JAXA's IBUKI GW tracks carbon dioxide, methane and nitrogen dioxide while also measuring the water cycle. Launched June 29, 2025 from Tanegashima Space Center. A satellite carrying TANSO-3 for greenhouse gases and AMSR3 for the water cycle.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech.

GRACE-FO (NASA / Germany)

Twin US-German satellites weigh Earth's water by measuring gravity, tracking groundwater, ice and sea level since 2018. Active. Twin satellites measuring Earth's changing gravity field.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/Goddard (GPM Core Observatory).

GSMaP Global Rainfall Map (JAXA)

JAXA's Global Satellite Mapping of Precipitation maps rain around the world every hour, used by 18,673 registered users in 163 countries and regions. A blend of microwave and infrared satellite data giving hourly global rainfall at about 11 x 11 km.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

HydroGNSS (ESA)

ESA's first Scout mission uses reflected navigation-satellite signals to measure soil moisture, wetlands and frozen ground (ESA). Two satellites that use GNSS reflectometry to measure soil moisture, freeze–thaw state over permafrost, flooding and wetlands, and biomass.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/Bill Ingalls.

ICESat-2 (NASA)

NASA's ICESat-2 laser fires 10,000 pulses a second to measure ice sheets, sea ice, forests and coastal seafloor in 3D. Active. A satellite laser altimeter measuring surface height in 3D.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA Earth Observatory (EO-1 ALI, July 17, 2011).

International Charter: Space and Major Disasters

When disaster strikes, 17 space agencies and satellite operators pool their satellites and give free images to rescue teams. Activated 1,000 times. A single 24/7 access point. When a disaster authority activates it, satellites are tasked and specialists turn the images into maps, free of charge to the user.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/Liz Wilk (KSC-20221109-PH-LNW01_0001).

Joint Polar Satellite System (NOAA)

NOAA's polar weather satellites circle Earth 14 times a day. Polar-orbiting satellites supply about 85% of the data used by weather prediction models. Satellites that measure atmospheric temperature and moisture, wind shear, land and sea surface temperature, clouds and rainfall rates.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (KSC-20210927-PH-KLS02_0069).

Landsat 9 (NASA / USGS)

Landsat 9 continues a 50-year record of Earth’s land, collecting 740 scenes a day and, with Landsat 8, seeing each place every 8 days. The latest Landsat satellite, collecting 740 scenes of Earth each day, almost double its minimum requirement.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/NOAA DSCOVR EPIC.

Libera Earth Energy Instrument (NASA)

Libera will measure how much energy leaves Earth each day, keeping a climate record that began in 2000 going strong (NOAA). An instrument that measures sunlight reflected by Earth and the infrared heat Earth gives off, from the top of the atmosphere.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (iss073e0080625, May 15, 2025).

Meteosat Third Generation (ESA / EUMETSAT)

Three weather satellites now watch Europe and Africa from 36,000 km up, with new images every 2.5 minutes and a camera that catches lightning day and night. Imager and Sounder satellites. The Imagers carry a Flexible Combined Imager and a Lightning Imager that sees lightning by day and night.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA26113).

Methane Alert and Response System (UNEP)

The UN's satellite methane alert system has sent more than 3,500 alerts about major leaks in 33 countries, and responses rose from 1% to 12% in a year. The first public global satellite system that detects very large methane emissions and notifies governments and companies, using more than 30 satellite instruments and SI models.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

MetOp-SG-A1 and Sentinel-5A (ESA / EUMETSAT)

Launched August 2025 on Ariane 6, MetOp-SG-A1 carries Sentinel-5, which delivers daily global data on air pollutants, ozone and climate gases. The first MetOp Second Generation weather satellite, carrying the Copernicus Sentinel-5 air-quality instrument.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech.

NISAR (NASA–ISRO)

NISAR, a NASA and ISRO radar satellite launched from India on July 30, 2025, scans nearly all of Earth's land and ice twice every 12 days, even through clouds. A radar satellite carrying NASA's L-band and ISRO's S-band radars.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech.

OCO-2 Orbiting Carbon Observatory (NASA)

NASA's OCO-2 satellite has measured carbon dioxide around the planet since 2014, showing where carbon is released and where forests and oceans absorb it. A satellite that measures carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, collecting around 100,000 measurements every day.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/KSC.

PACE (NASA)

NASA's PACE reads the color of the ocean to track phytoplankton, the base of the marine food web, plus aerosols and clouds. A satellite whose hyperspectral Ocean Color Instrument measures light from ultraviolet to near-infrared.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA, ISS Expedition 23 crew (iss023e057948).

PREFIRE Polar CubeSats (NASA)

Two shoebox-size NASA satellites measure the far-infrared heat that Earth's poles send into space (NASA JPL). Last reviewed: October 2026 · 4 sources cited Two CubeSats that measure far-infrared heat, showing how water vapor, clouds and ice trap heat or let it escape to space.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS070-E-086805, Feb. 3, 2024).

RADARSAT Constellation Mission (Canada)

Three Canadian radar satellites watch Canada's land, coasts and Arctic day and night, through cloud and smoke, with about 250,000 images a year. Three identical C-band synthetic aperture radar satellites. Each carries a ship-tracking (AIS) receiver.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/KSC.

SARSAT Search and Rescue Satellites (NOAA)

NOAA's SARSAT satellites relay distress beacon signals to rescuers; the international system has supported more than 50,000 rescues worldwide since 1982 (NOAA). Satellites that detect signals from emergency beacons on boats, planes and hikers and relay their location.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL (PIA23534).

SMAP Soil Moisture Active Passive (NASA)

SMAP maps the water in Earth's soil from space, helping improve predictions of weather, floods and droughts (NASA). A satellite with a radar, a radiometer and the largest rotating mesh antenna ever deployed in space, 20 feet (6 meters) wide.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech.

SWOT (NASA–CNES)

SWOT measures more than 95% of Earth's lakes larger than 15 acres and rivers wider than 330 feet, the first global survey of fresh water. A radar interferometer satellite that measures the height of water on land and at sea.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (Carbon Mapper data).

Tanager-1 (Carbon Mapper / Planet)

Tanager-1, built by Planet with a NASA JPL-designed spectrometer for the nonprofit Carbon Mapper, pinpoints methane and CO2 plumes from single sources. A satellite that pinpoints methane and carbon dioxide plumes from individual sources.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

TEMPO Air Quality Mission (NASA)

From geostationary orbit, NASA's TEMPO measures air pollution over North America every daylight hour, a first from space (NASA). TEMPO, a spectrometer that measures air pollutants such as ozone and nitrogen dioxide.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS074-E-0046371, Jan. 3, 2026).

The GLOBE Program

A worldwide science program where students and citizen scientists collect real environmental data: more than 293 million measurements from 127 countries. People worldwide take environmental measurements, such as clouds, trees, land cover and soil, and share them for science.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA26652).

The Science of Tsunami Detection

Deep-ocean buoys, satellites, GPS signals and seafloor cables spot tsunamis in the open ocean so coastal communities get time to reach high ground (NOAA, NASA). A chain of sensors that measures the ocean itself, not just the shaking ground, so forecasters can confirm a tsunami and estimate its size.

Also in: Ocean

Read the 5 W's

Hub Summary

Satellites that map moving ground, forest carbon, methane leaks and the whole sunlit Earth.

You can't protect what you can't measure, and much of this data is free for anyone to use.

Impact: You can't protect what you can't measure. These satellites map moving ground, forest carbon, methane leaks and the ozone over the whole planet, and much of their data is free for anyone to use.

Forests (Top)

Image: SI Partner.

American Forests

Since 1875, American Forests has worked to restore U.S. forests, from big natural landscapes to city streets, with more than 63 million trees planted. Forest restoration, urban forestry and Tree Equity, which aims to bring trees to every neighborhood.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (STS-72).

Eden: People+Planet

Eden: People+Planet restores landscapes with local communities, including a 394,000-hectare project in Tanzania's Rubeho Mountains (Eden). Community-led landscape restoration that combines protecting existing forest with planting new trees, funded by donations together with verified carbon credits.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS056-E-127068, Aug. 6, 2018).

European Forest Fire Information System (EFFIS)

Europe's wildfire system forecasts fire danger and maps burnt areas from satellites. In 2024 it mapped wildfires in 42 countries. Fire danger forecasts, active fire detection and rapid satellite mapping of burnt areas, using MODIS, VIIRS and Sentinel-2 images.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

GEDI Forest Lidar (NASA)

GEDI fires lasers from the International Space Station to measure the height and 3D shape of Earth's forests (University of Maryland). A lidar with 3 lasers that make 8 parallel tracks of measurements. Each laser spot is 25 meters wide, and GEDI measures canopy height, canopy structure and ground elevation.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

Global Forest Watch (WRI)

Free satellite forest data and deforestation alerts from WRI; where Indigenous forest monitors used it, deforestation fell 52% in one year. A free online map with forest data, weekly deforestation alerts and a field app called Forest Watcher.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (iss012e11654).

Great Green Wall (African Union / UNCCD)

An African-led effort to restore land across the Sahel. A 2020 UN status report counted about 4 million hectares restored inside the Great Green Wall zone. A mosaic of restored land, trees, farms and pastures stretching across Africa.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (STS072-722-004).

Justdiggit

With farmers and pastoralists in Africa, Justdiggit is restoring 525,000 hectares of dry land and has brought back 32.3 million trees (organization figures). Rainwater harvesting with "Earth Smile" bunds and regrowing trees from old stumps (farmer managed natural regeneration).

Read the 5 W's

Photo: SI Partner.

Life Terra

Life Terra and its partners have planted more than 36 million trees across Europe (company figure), working toward a goal of 500 million trees. Community-based tree planting paired with an app and monitoring technology that registers every tree and tracks its growth. They run large professional plantings, local volunteer events, school climate education, and corporate tree-adoption programs.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA.

Mangrove Forests

About 80 species of mangrove trees guard tropical coastlines: their tangled roots hold mud in place, soften storm surges and give fish food and shelter. Salt-tolerant coastal forests that grow in low-oxygen, waterlogged soil.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech.

One Tree Planted

Since 2014, One Tree Planted has planted more than 171 million trees with 489 partners in 84 countries (organization figure). Reforestation and agroforestry projects, each followed by three years of monitoring to check tree survival.

Read the 5 W's

Image: SI Partner.

Plant-for-the-Planet

Started by a 9-year-old in 2007, Plant-for-the-Planet trains young Climate Justice Ambassadors and restores forests, including Mexico's Mayan forests. Climate education for children and youth, plus forest restoration and a free platform for funding and monitoring restoration projects.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS013-E-74843).

Rainforest Connection

Solar-powered Guardian listening devices and SI detect chainsaws in real time; Rainforest Connection reports more than 726,000 hectares of rainforest protected. Solar-powered Guardian devices high in trees that listen to the forest and use SI to spot threats.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (Danube Delta, where the Danube meets the Black Sea).

Rewilding Europe

Rewilding Europe helps wild nature come back across Europe. Rewilding Europe, working with local partners in large pioneering landscapes. Rewilding: bringing back wildlife and natural processes such as grazing and free-flowing rivers, and supporting nature-based businesses.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center.

Saharan Dust Feeds the Amazon

Every year about 27.7 million tons of Saharan dust falls on the Amazon, delivering some 22,000 tons of phosphorus that the rainforest needs (NASA). Wind carries phosphorus-rich dust from the Sahara across the Atlantic to fertilize the Amazon.

Read the 5 W's

Photo: SI Partner.

Terraformation

Terraformation grows native, biodiverse forests from seed to canopy across the tropics and Hawaii, funding seed collection, nurseries, planting and monitoring. Restoring degraded land into native, biodiverse forests that capture carbon and support communities. Projects use up to five times more native species than typical carbon-offset plantings, tracked with open-source software from nursery to canopy.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS013-E-74843).

The Amazon Makes Its Own Rainy Season

Water breathed out by Amazon trees starts the rainy season two to three months before winds alone would bring it (NASA JPL). Transpiration: trees pull water from the ground and release vapor that builds rain clouds.

Read the 5 W's

Photo: SI Partner.

Three Trees for Every One Removed

Saving Planets' own forest rule: plant three trees for every one removed, so each removal becomes a net gain for habitat, soil and shade, and is easy to verify. For every tree that comes out, three go in. That replaces what was lost and starts to rebuild habitat, soil, and shade.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (STS072-722-004).

Trees for the Future

Partnering with East African farmers on regenerative farms: 450M+ trees grown and 99,000 acres restored (nonprofit figures). Regenerative farming with diverse trees and plants to restore soil, biodiversity and harvests.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (STS-72).

WeForest

WeForest has grown more than 118 million trees and has 100,000 hectares of forest under restoration and conservation (WeForest). It restores and conserves forests at the landscape level, to get the most out of trees for people, nature and climate.

Read the 5 W's

Hub Summary

Planting and tracking trees, and restoring native forests.

Forests hold soil, shade land and give habitat. Replanting more than is lost rebuilds them.

Impact: Forests come back when people plant and protect them. Life Terra reports more than 36 million trees planted with partners.

Lakes (Top)

Image: NASA Goddard (Great Lakes, No Clouds, August 28, 2010).

Adopt-a-Beach (Alliance for the Great Lakes)

Each year, Adopt-a-Beach volunteers remove tens of thousands of pounds of litter from Great Lakes beaches, about 85% of it plastic. Beach cleanups where volunteers pick up litter and record what they find.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS071-E-14284), via Wikimedia Commons, public domain.

Clean Up the Lake

Clean Up the Lake removes submerged litter from freshwater lakes using scuba divers and volunteers, and GPS-pins, sorts and weighs every piece it finds. Underwater and shoreline cleanups of freshwater lakes, with every piece of litter GPS-pinned, categorized, and weighed. They also run aquatic invasive species monitoring and youth education programs.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA GSFC MODIS Land Rapid Response Team (July 4, 2020), via Wikimedia Commons, public domain.

Great Lakes Plastic Cleanup

The Great Lakes Plastic Cleanup captures plastic across the world's largest freshwater system using Seabins, drones, and beach robots. Deploying proven capture technology — Seabins, PixieDrones, BeBots, and stormwater LittaTraps — to remove plastic from marinas, shorelines, and drains before it reaches open water, plus community science to characterize what is found.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS071-E-14284).

Keep Tahoe Blue (League to Save Lake Tahoe)

Keep Tahoe Blue, the League to Save Lake Tahoe, has protected the lake for decades, and its volunteers picked up 1,330 pounds of litter after July 4, 2026. Science-based protection of Lake Tahoe, community cleanups and advocacy.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA Goddard (Great Lakes, No Clouds, August 28, 2010).

Lake Erie Harmful Algal Bloom Forecast (NOAA)

NOAA forecasts and tracks toxic algae in western Lake Erie through the summer so cities and swimmers can keep drinking water and people safe (NOAA NCCOS). A seasonal forecast plus regular bulletins that show where the cyanobacteria bloom is and where it is moving.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NPS photo, Lake Roosevelt National Recreation Area (NPGallery), via Wikimedia Commons, public domain.

Lake Plastic Recovery

Saving Planets' own lake solution: recover plastic from freshwater lakes by skimming, shoreline collection and divers, before it travels downstream to the sea. Pulling plastic and litter out of lakes — surface skimming, shoreline collection, and diver-assisted recovery — so it never reaches rivers and oceans.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (ISS071-E-14284).

Living Lakes (Global Nature Fund)

Since 1998, the Global Nature Fund's Living Lakes Network has protected and restored lakes and wetlands with 130 member organizations in more than 60 countries. Protecting and restoring lakes and wetlands: removing plastic waste from waterways, pushing for strong protections and creating sustainable incomes for local communities.

Read the 5 W's

Created by SI and Biological SI Partner.

Restoring the Lake Depths Foundation

A Nevada nonprofit whose ROVs and volunteer divers pull submerged trash from Lake Tahoe, from the surface down to 1,640 feet: over 13 tons in summer 2024 alone. A fleet of four ROVs — including the flagship Deep Emerald with a 600-meter fiber optic tether — plus volunteer scuba and free divers, removing submerged debris from Lake Tahoe's depths. They also provide high-resolution underwater surveying photography and videography.

Read the 5 W's

Hub Summary

Removing submerged and floating litter from freshwater lakes with divers, ROVs, Seabins and drones.

Litter that stays in a lake can break down there or travel downstream into rivers and the ocean. Recovering it at the lake stops both.

Impact: Lakes catch litter before it moves downstream. Clean Up The Lake has logged 36,177 pounds (about 16,400 kg) of litter removed since 2018.

Ocean (Top)

Created by SI and Biological SI Partner.

4ocean

4ocean removes plastic from oceans, rivers, and coastlines with full-time cleanup crews, funded by products made from recovered plastic. Full-time professional captains and cleanup crews recover plastic and trash from oceans, rivers, and coastlines every day. The model: each bracelet or product sold funds the removal of one pound of trash, and recovered material is sorted, shredded, and turned into recycled products.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NOAA (Deep SOLO float deployment from R/V Kaʻimikai-O-Kanaloa, 2018).

Argo Ocean Float Array

Close to 4,000 robotic floats dive through the ocean and send back temperature and salinity data, free to everyone. The 3 millionth profile came in 2024. Robotic floats that dive as deep as 2,000 m (Deep Argo floats go to 6,000 m) and drift for 10 days between reports, producing 100,000+ profiles a year.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (2010-5237).

Argos Satellite Tracking System

A satellite system that tracks wildlife and ocean instruments from tiny transmitters, following more than 8,000 animals every month for conservation science. Instruments from CNES fly on NOAA, EUMETSAT and ISRO polar-orbiting satellites and pick up signals from small transmitters on animals, buoys and floats.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (STS039-88-054).

Billion Oyster Project

A New York nonprofit restoring the harbor with oyster reefs, aiming for one billion oysters by 2030 (nonprofit figures). Oyster reef restoration, shell recycling and harbor education.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/GSFC/Jeff Schmaltz/MODIS Land Rapid Response Team.

Copernicus Marine Service

Free, open ocean data and forecasts for the whole planet. In 2025 users downloaded almost 15,000 terabytes of ocean data from Copernicus Marine. Free, open, science-based information on the physical ocean, sea ice and ocean biogeochemistry, from global to regional scales.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA (STS093-717-066, coral reefs of Bora Bora, French Polynesia).

Coral Gardeners

Coral Gardeners grows corals in nurseries and replants them on reefs in French Polynesia, Fiji and Thailand: 238,250 corals planted so far. Growing coral fragments in nurseries and replanting them on damaged reefs, using new tools and methods.

Read the 5 W's

Image: NASA/JPL.

Coral IVF (Great Barrier Reef Foundation)

Researchers capture coral spawn and rear millions of baby corals for damaged reefs; the first Coral IVF corals reproduced on the Great Barrier Reef in 2021. Collecting coral eggs and sperm during spawning and rearing baby corals in floating pools and tanks before placing them on damaged reefs.

Read the 5 W's
