Klamath River Dam Removal

Four Klamath River dams came out by October 2024, reopening more than 400 miles of habitat to salmon, steelhead and lamprey (Klamath River Renewal).

Image: NASA (PIA26448, Klamath River, Oregon and California).

  1. Home
  2. Rivers
  3. Klamath River Dam Removal

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 3 sources cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • Dams are walls across rivers. On the Klamath River, four big dams stopped salmon from swimming upstream for about 100 years.
  • In 2024 all four dams were taken out.
  • Now salmon can swim to more than 400 miles of river again, and people are planting new plants where the old lakes were.

The Five W's of Klamath River Dam Removal

Who

The Klamath River Renewal Corporation, Tribal Nations, the states of Oregon and California, and NOAA Fisheries.

Source: NOAA Fisheries, Final step in Klamath River dam removal

What

The removal of four hydroelectric dams on the lower Klamath River, the largest dam removal in U.S. history.

Source: Klamath River Renewal Corporation, Klamath dam removal comes to a close

When

The smallest dam came out in 2023; the last work in the river finished on October 2, 2024.

Source: Office of the Governor of California, Klamath River dams fully removed

Where

The Klamath River in southern Oregon and northern California.

Source: Klamath River Renewal Corporation, Klamath dam removal comes to a close

Why

The dams blocked salmon for about a century and warmed the river. The Klamath was once the third-largest salmon river on the West Coast.

Source: Office of the Governor of California, Klamath River dams fully removed

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

With the dams gone, fish can again reach more than 400 miles of river and creeks in the upper Klamath Basin. Crews are replanting 2,200 acres of land that sat underwater for a century.

Source: Klamath River Renewal Corporation, Klamath dam removal comes to a close

For Further Information: NOAA Fisheries, Final step in Klamath River dam removal

Removing four Klamath River dams reopened more than 400 miles of habitat to salmon.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Tribal Nations and river communities depend on Klamath salmon for food and culture; reopened habitat gives the fishery a path to recovery.

Source: Office of the Governor of California, Klamath River dams fully removed

For Nature

Salmon, steelhead and lamprey can swim to habitat blocked for decades, and native seed now covers the drained reservoir land.

Source: NOAA Fisheries, Final step in Klamath River dam removal
