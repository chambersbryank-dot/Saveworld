The Ocean Cleanup

The largest cleanup in history — removing plastic from our oceans and rivers.

The Five W's of The Ocean Cleanup

Who

The Ocean Cleanup, a nonprofit founded by Boyan Slat. Engineers, scientists, and volunteers working across more than a dozen countries to remove plastic from oceans and rivers.

What

They develop and scale technologies that remove legacy plastic from garbage patches and coastal waters, intercept plastic flowing from rivers before it reaches the sea, and advocate for international regulations like a Global Plastics Treaty.

When

Active for over ten years, with operations ongoing. Key milestones include breaking ten million kilograms removed in April twenty twenty-four and fifteen million kilograms by July twenty twenty-four.

Where

Deployed globally: the Great Pacific Garbage Patch, coastal ecosystems, and around one thousand rivers worldwide that contribute eighty percent of riverine plastic pollution — including Indonesia, Thailand, Guatemala, Jamaica, Panama, Malaysia, and Los Angeles County.

Why

Plastic pollution is a worsening crisis. Trillions of pieces harm marine wildlife, degrade into microplastics that enter the human food chain, and persist for decades. Cleanup protects ecosystems, communities, and the water we all depend on.

Impact Statement

Tens of millions of kilograms of plastic have been removed from the world's oceans and rivers to date. Their offshore systems have completed missions in the Great Pacific Garbage Patch, offloading up to fifty-five metric tons in a single extraction. River Interceptors have caught over two million kilograms in Guatemala alone and more than one and a half million in Thailand. The mission: clean up ninety percent of floating ocean plastic by twenty forty.

This is what an honest, hopeful approach looks like — using the same engineering that built the modern world to restore it, without guilt and without politics. Crystal clear water is the goal.
