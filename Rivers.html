Rivers

Impact Statement

Rivers carry plastic from land to the sea, so catching it in the river is one of the most direct fixes. This hub covers The Ocean Cleanup's Interceptors, Sungai Watch's trash barriers in Indonesia and The Great Bubble Barrier in the Netherlands. Each solution page lists its own sourced figures.

The Five W's of River Solutions

Who

The Ocean Cleanup (nonprofit), Sungai Watch (nonprofit) and The Great Bubble Barrier (social enterprise).

What

Barriers, bubble curtains and solar-powered Interceptors that stop floating plastic in rivers and canals.

When

Ongoing.

Where

Rivers and canals in Indonesia, the Netherlands and other countries with Interceptors.

Why

Stopping plastic upstream is cheaper and easier than collecting it once it has spread across the ocean.

Missions and Solutions

FreshWater Watch (Earthwatch Europe)

Earthwatch Europe's citizen science project testing water quality worldwide; 80,000+ datasets from 22,000+ volunteers (organization figures).

River Cleanup

River Cleanup and its volunteers have recovered more than 4.39 million kg of waste from rivers worldwide and aim for 1,000 plastic-free rivers by 2050 (organization figures).

SI-generated image by Saving Planets.

American Rivers

American Rivers has worked directly on more than 200 dam removals; in 2025 the U.S. removed 100 dams and reconnected more than 4,893 river miles (American Rivers).

Living Lands & Waters

A barge-based river cleanup crew that, with more than 141,000 volunteers, has pulled over 14 million pounds of trash from U.S. rivers (organization figure).

SI-generated image by Saving Planets.

Elwha River Restoration

Two Elwha River dams came down between 2011 and 2014, and more than 20 million tons of trapped sediment flowed downstream to rebuild the river and coast (NPS, USGS).

Klamath River Dam Removal

Four Klamath River dams came out by October 2024, reopening more than 400 miles of habitat to salmon, steelhead and lamprey (Klamath River Renewal).

SI-generated image by Saving Planets.

Mr. Trash Wheel Family

Beavers, River Engineers

In Oregon's Bridge Creek, beavers given simple dam starters raised juvenile steelhead survival 52% and production 175% (NOAA Fisheries).
