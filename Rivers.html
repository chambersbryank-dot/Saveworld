River Interceptors

The solution

About 1,000 rivers carry most of the plastic that reaches the ocean. Interceptors catch it in the river before it gets there. The Ocean Cleanup runs this work — the same organization that cleans the Great Pacific Garbage Patch.

The scale

Their research found roughly 1,000 rivers out of three million carry about 80 percent of riverine plastic. Interceptors are solar-powered, autonomous, and can store up to 50 cubic meters of trash before needing to be emptied.

Where they work

As of 2026, about 26 Interceptors operate across 12 countries — Indonesia, Malaysia, Vietnam, Thailand, India, the Philippines, Guatemala, Honduras, Panama, the Dominican Republic, Jamaica, and the United States, including Ballona Creek in Los Angeles.

The bigger plan

Their 30 Cities Program aims to eliminate up to one-third of all plastic flowing from rivers into the ocean before the end of the decade. In 2025 they recorded their largest river catch to date — 27.3 million kilograms, a 131 percent increase over 2024.
