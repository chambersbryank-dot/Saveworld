Amazing Facts That Matter

Numbers behind the work. Sources belong on each fact.

Banner: composite of NASA images — Earthset from Orion (Artemis II), Hubble eXtreme Deep Field (NASA/ESA), Neptune by Voyager 2 (NASA/JPL)

150 Times Safer

Image: NASA

ESA's first-of-its-kind assisted reentry of the Aeolus satellite cut the already low risk from falling debris by a factor of 150.

From: Aeolus Assisted Reentry (ESA)

Source: ESA, Final images before Aeolus demise

49 Flybys of an Ocean Moon

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech

NASA's Europa Clipper will travel 1.8 billion miles to Jupiter and fly past the ocean moon Europa 49 times.

From: Europa Clipper (NASA)

Source: NASA, Europa Clipper

A Coronagraph That Never Blinks

Image: NASA/KSC

GOES-19's CCOR-1 is the world's first operational space-based coronagraph, imaging the Sun's faint corona every 15 minutes.

From: GOES-19 and CCOR-1 (NOAA)

Source: NOAA SWPC, CCOR-1 coronagraph

A Dozen Photons Come Home

Image: NASA/Bill Ingalls

ICESat-2's laser fires 10,000 pulses a second, each with about 300 trillion photons, and only about a dozen return to the satellite.

From: ICESat-2 (NASA)

Source: NASA, ICESat-2

A Half-Hour Warning From a Million Miles

Image: NASA/KSC

From about a million miles toward the Sun, NASA's IMAP can give astronauts and spacecraft near Earth about a half hour's warning of incoming radiation.

From: IMAP (NASA)

Source: NASA, IMAP mission

A Million Lakes From Orbit

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech

NASA–CNES SWOT keeps watch on the water levels of more than a million lakes larger than 15 acres.

From: SWOT (NASA–CNES)

Source: NASA Earthdata, SWOT data release

A Quarter-Million Corals Replanted

Image: NASA/JPL

Coral Restoration Foundation had returned 253,833 nursery-grown corals to Florida's reefs by the end of 2024 (organization figure).

From: Coral Restoration Foundation

Source: Coral Restoration Foundation, 2024 annual report (organization figure)

A Satellite Made of Wood

Image: Kyoto University via NASA

LignoSat, the world's first wooden satellite, was built from magnolia wood panels joined with a traditional Japanese technique that uses no glue or nails.

From: LignoSat Wooden Satellite (Kyoto University)

Source: NASA, JAXA’s first wooden satellite deploys

Closer Than Our Satellites

Image: NASA

On April 13, 2029, asteroid Apophis will pass about 20,000 miles from Earth, closer than many geosynchronous satellites.

From: OSIRIS-APEX and Apophis 2029 (NASA)

Source: NASA, Asteroid Apophis

Clouds in Motion

Image: NASA

EarthCARE carries the first cloud radar in space that can measure how fast cloud particles move up and down.

From: EarthCARE (ESA–JAXA)

Source: JAXA, EarthCARE Cloud Profiling Radar

Coastal Bodyguards

Image: NASA

There are about 80 species of mangrove trees, and their tangled roots help shield coastlines from storm surges.

From: Mangrove Forests

Source: NOAA, What is a mangrove forest?

Earth's Ultraviolet Halo

Image: NASA/KSC

Earth wears a faint ultraviolet halo, the geocorona, first photographed from the Moon by Apollo 16 in 1972; NASA's Carruthers mission now charts it from a million miles away.

From: Carruthers Geocorona Observatory (NASA)

Source: NASA, Carruthers Geocorona Observatory

Eighteen Inches a Day

Image: NASA (ISS018-E-018110)

In ideal conditions, kelp can grow up to 18 inches in a single day.

From: Kelp Forests

Source: NOAA, What is a kelp forest?

Every Other Breath

Image: NASA

About half of Earth's oxygen comes from the ocean, most of it made by tiny phytoplankton.

From: Phytoplankton, the Ocean's Oxygen Engine

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

Farthest Humans Have Gone

Image: NASA

The Artemis II crew reached 252,756 miles from Earth on April 6, 2026, the farthest humans have ever traveled.

From: Artemis II: The Farthest Humans Have Gone

Source: NASA, Artemis II crew back on Earth

Five Hundred Tons a Year

Image: NASA

Baltimore's four Trash Wheels collect about 500 tons of trash and debris from the harbor each year (organization figure).

From: Mr. Trash Wheel Family

Source: Mr. Trash Wheel, trash interception (organization figure)

One Thousand Rivers

Image: NASA/USGS Landsat, Lena Delta

About 1,000 rivers contribute most riverine plastic reaching the ocean, per a Science Advances riverine-plastic study.

Source: Meijer et al., Science Advances 2021

Room For 1,300 Earths

Image: NASA/JPL/University of Arizona, Cassini

You could fit roughly 1,300 Earths inside Jupiter (about 1,321 by volume).

From: Jupiter: Earth's Natural Debris Shield

Source: NASA Jupiter Fact Sheet

Sixty-Five Million Kilograms

Image: NASA, International Space Station

The Ocean Cleanup reported 65 million kilograms of plastic removed as of October 2026 — their biggest year ever was 2025, with 27 million kilograms alone.

Source: The Ocean Cleanup (organization figure) · 2025 in review

Small Meadows, Big Carbon

Image: NASA/KSC

Seagrass covers just 0.1% of the world's seafloor but stores 11% of the organic carbon buried in the ocean.

From: Seagrass Meadows

Source: NOAA, Blue carbon fast facts
