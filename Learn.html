Learn

Three tracks, six levels: from a child’s first question about orbits to research-grade derivations. The math is taught inside each track, step by step.

Tracks

  • Available now

    Orbital Mechanics

    How satellites and spacecraft move: Kepler, vis-viva, transfers, perturbations and Lambert targeting.

    • Orbits explained (kids, teens, college)
    • Orbital mechanics math, step by step (undergraduate to PhD)
    • Orbit Lab: plan a transfer
  • Coming next

    Aeronautical Engineering

    How aircraft fly: lift and drag, airfoils, propulsion, performance, stability and control of high-performance jets and rotorcraft.

    Planned: atmosphere and units; lift, drag and the airfoil (thin-airfoil theory); finite wings (lifting-line); compressible flow and shocks; jet propulsion (Brayton cycle); aircraft performance; stability and control (linearised state-space); rotorcraft (momentum and blade-element theory); CFD basics.

  • Coming next

    Physics

    The foundations: classical mechanics, Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics and statistical mechanics, fluids, and the relativity that matters for orbits.

    Planned: Newtonian mechanics and conservation laws; Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics (central-force problem, Noether); electromagnetism (Maxwell’s equations, plasma and space weather); thermodynamics and statistical mechanics; fluid mechanics (Navier–Stokes); special and general relativity for GPS clocks and Mercury’s perihelion.

Levels

  • Kids
  • Teens
  • Community college
  • Undergraduate (freshman–senior)
  • Graduate
  • PhD / research

How every lesson is built

  • Clear learning objectives
  • Prerequisites stated up front
  • Step-by-step derivations, with the math method named (calculus, vectors, ODEs, numerical methods)
  • Worked examples, then practice problems with solutions
  • Common mistakes to avoid
  • Cited textbooks and papers; every number computed and checked
