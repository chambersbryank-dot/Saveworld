Aerospace, Space and Earth-Science Careers: Pay, Outlook and How to Get There

Share to Google Classroom(opens in a new tab)

Real jobs, the steps to reach them, and the math that matters. U.S. figures from the Bureau of Labor Statistics; paths differ in other countries.

Facts checked: 2026-10-16

Pay and outlook: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook (pages last modified August 27, 2026). Pay is the May 2025 median; outlook covers 2025–35.

  • ✈️ Aerospace engineer (aeronautics)
  • 🛰️ Astronautical engineer (spacecraft and rockets)
  • 🛫 Pilot (civil and military)
  • 🔧 A&P mechanic (aircraft maintenance)
  • 🚁 Drone / UAS pilot
  • 🔭 Astrophysicist / physicist
  • 📡 Space operations (satellites and launch)
  • 🌊 Environmental scientist / oceanographer

✈️ Aerospace engineer (aeronautics)

What they do: Design, analyse and test aircraft, engines and their systems: wings, structures, propulsion and flight controls.

Path from high school: High school: math up to calculus, physics, chemistry. Then a bachelor’s degree in aerospace engineering or a related engineering field (BLS: typical entry education). Internships and co-ops help a lot.

Math and physics to learn here: Aeronautical engineering math · Lift Equation Calculator · Stall Speed Calculator · Mach Number & Speed of Sound Calculator

  • BLSAerospace engineers
  • U.S. median pay (May 2025)$134,960
  • Projected growth 2025–35+8% · 3,800 openings per year

🛰️ Astronautical engineer (spacecraft and rockets)

What they do: Design spacecraft, launch vehicles and missions: trajectories, propulsion, guidance and the systems that keep a satellite working. BLS counts them inside aerospace engineers.

Path from high school: Same as aerospace engineering, often with an astronautics or space-systems focus. Student rocketry, CubeSat teams and NASA internships are common first steps.

Math and physics to learn here: Orbital mechanics math · Delta-v Calculator · Hohmann Transfer Calculator · Orbit Lab

  • BLSAerospace engineers
  • U.S. median pay (May 2025)$134,960
  • Projected growth 2025–35+8% · 3,800 openings per year

🛫 Pilot (civil and military)

What they do: Fly aircraft for airlines, cargo, charter, firefighting, survey or the armed forces; plan flights, check weather and manage the aircraft’s performance.

Path from high school: Civil: FAA student, private and commercial pilot certificates, an instrument rating and flight hours; airline captains need an Airline Transport Pilot certificate. Military: become an officer (service academy, ROTC or officer training), then the service’s own flight training. See the FAA’s “Become a Pilot” page.

Math and physics to learn here: How Flight Works · Stall Speed Calculator · Lift Equation Calculator · Mach Number & Speed of Sound Calculator

  • BLSAirline and commercial pilots
  • U.S. median pay (May 2025)$210,020
  • Projected growth 2025–35+7% · 16,400 openings per year

🔧 A&P mechanic (aircraft maintenance)

What they do: Inspect, repair and sign off airframes and powerplants (A&P) so aircraft are safe to fly.

Path from high school: High school diploma, then an FAA-certificated aviation maintenance technician school (Part 147) or documented experience, then the FAA written, oral and practical tests. See the FAA’s “Become a Mechanic” page.

Math and physics to learn here: How Flight Works · Thrust-to-Weight Ratio Calculator · Physics Explained

  • BLSAircraft and avionics equipment mechanics and technicians
  • U.S. median pay (May 2025)$80,180
  • Projected growth 2025–35+6% · 13,100 openings per year

🚁 Drone / UAS pilot

What they do: Fly uncrewed aircraft for mapping, inspection, agriculture, film, public safety and research.

Path from high school: In the U.S., flying drones for work needs the FAA Remote Pilot Certificate (Part 107), earned by passing the FAA knowledge test. See “Become a Drone Pilot”. Community-college UAS programs add mapping and data skills.

Math and physics to learn here: Aeronautical engineering math · Rotor Hover Power Calculator · Aero Lab

BLS does not publish a separate occupation for this job.

🔭 Astrophysicist / physicist

What they do: Study how matter, energy, gravity and light behave, from lab experiments to stars and galaxies; build models and instruments.

Path from high school: Physics and calculus in high school, a bachelor’s in physics or astronomy, then usually a Ph.D. for research jobs (BLS lists a doctoral degree as typical entry education).

Math and physics to learn here: Physics math · Orbital Period Calculator · Pendulum Period Calculator · Projectile Motion Calculator with Air Resistance

  • BLSPhysicists and astronomers
  • U.S. median pay (May 2025)$166,880
  • Projected growth 2025–35+7% · 1,500 openings per year

📡 Space operations (satellites and launch)

What they do: Run satellites and launches from control centres: plan passes and manoeuvres, watch spacecraft health, track objects and avoid collisions. Done by companies, agencies and the U.S. Space Force.

Path from high school: Routes include an associate’s degree as an aerospace engineering and operations technician (the BLS figures shown are for that related occupation), a bachelor’s in engineering, physics or computer science, or military service.

Math and physics to learn here: Orbital mechanics math · Orbital Period Calculator · Orbital Velocity & Escape Velocity Calculator · Satellite Explorer

BLS does not publish a separate occupation for this job.

  • Closest BLS occupationAerospace engineering and operations technicians
  • U.S. median pay (May 2025)$82,890
  • Projected growth 2025–35+12% · 1,100 openings per year

🌊 Environmental scientist / oceanographer

What they do: Measure and protect air, water, land and oceans, often with satellite data, field work and computer models. BLS groups oceanographers with geoscientists.

Path from high school: Biology, chemistry, physics and statistics in high school; a bachelor’s in environmental science, geoscience, oceanography or a related science; a master’s helps for many roles.

Math and physics to learn here: Satellite Explorer · Orbits Explained · Physics math · Spot the Fire

  • BLSEnvironmental scientists and specialists
  • U.S. median pay (May 2025)$82,220
  • Projected growth 2025–35+6% · 7,300 openings per year
  • BLSGeoscientists
  • U.S. median pay (May 2025)$101,920
  • Projected growth 2025–35+5% · 1,800 openings per year

Community college: a strong, affordable start

Many paths start at a community college: an associate’s degree for aerospace technicians (BLS: typical entry education), FAA Part 147 maintenance programs, drone (UAS) programs, and transfer tracks into four-year engineering and science degrees. Finish calculus and physics there, then transfer.

Veterans and service members

  • Post-9/11 GI Bill (VA): tuition, housing and books for eligible veterans and service members; it can cover degrees, flight training and some certifications.
  • Edith Nourse Rogers STEM Scholarship (VA): extra benefits for eligible students in STEM degrees when GI Bill entitlement runs low.
  • DoD SkillBridge: lets eligible service members train or intern with civilian employers during their last months of service.
