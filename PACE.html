PACE (NASA)

NASA's PACE reads the color of the ocean to track phytoplankton, the base of the marine food web, plus aerosols and clouds.

Image: NASA/KSC.

  1. Home
  2. Earth Observation
  3. PACE (NASA)

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 2 sources cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • What is it? A satellite whose hyperspectral Ocean Color Instrument measures light from ultraviolet to near-infrared.
  • Why does it matter? Tiny plant-like living things that drift in water and use sunlight to make food. They make much of the oxygen we breathe and feed ocean life. Glossary feed ocean life and help move carbon; tracking them shows how the ocean and climate are changing.

The Five W's of PACE

Who

NASA, built and operated by Goddard Space Flight Center.

Source: NASA, PACE mission

What

A satellite whose hyperspectral Ocean Color Instrument measures light from ultraviolet to near-infrared.

Source: NASA, PACE launch

When

Launched February 8, 2024 on a Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral.

Source: NASA, PACE launch

Where

Orbiting 676 km (420 mi) above Earth, circling the planet about every 98.3 minutes.

Source: NASA, PACE mission

Why

Phytoplankton feed ocean life and help move carbon; tracking them shows how the ocean and climate are changing.

Source: NASA, PACE mission

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

PACE (Plankton, Tiny bits of dust, smoke, salt or pollution floating in the air. They can block sunlight and help clouds form. Glossary, Cloud, ocean A community of living things and the place they live, like a reef or a forest, all depending on each other. Glossary) launched on February 8, 2024. Its hyperspectral Ocean Color Instrument tracks phytoplankton, tiny organisms that start the marine food web and drive the ocean's carbon cycle, and it shows how aerosols may feed their growth.

Source: NASA, PACE launch

Source: NASA, PACE mission
