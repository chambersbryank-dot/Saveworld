GEDI Forest Lidar (NASA)

GEDI fires lasers from the International Space Station to measure the height and 3D shape of Earth's forests (University of Maryland).

Image: NASA.

  1. Home

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 4 sources cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • GEDI is a laser tool on the International Space Station.
  • It shines light down on forests and times how long it takes to bounce back.
  • That tells scientists how tall trees are and how much carbon forests hold.

The Five W's of GEDI

Who

The University of Maryland leads GEDI with NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center. NASA selected it as an Earth Venture Instrument mission in 2014.

Source: University of Maryland, GEDI mission overview

What

A lidar with 3 lasers that make 8 parallel tracks of measurements. Each laser spot is 25 meters wide, and GEDI measures canopy height, canopy structure and ground elevation.

Source: University of Maryland, GEDI mission overview

When

GEDI was stored on the station from March 2023, reinstalled in April 2024 and returned to forest monitoring that spring.

Source: NASA, Return of the GEDI (May 2024)

Where

On the International Space Station, observing nearly all of Earth's tropical and temperate forests.

Source: NASA, Return of the GEDI (May 2024)

Why

Forests are changing fast. GEDI's 3D measurements help track forest carbon, The total amount of living material, like the wood and leaves in a forest. Measuring it shows how much carbon plants store. Glossary and habitat.

Source: University of Maryland, GEDI mission overview

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

GEDI has produced more than 10 billion high-quality observations of forest structure (University of Maryland).

Source: University of Maryland, GEDI mission overview

For Further Information: University of Maryland, GEDI mission overview

Each GEDI laser spot on the ground is 25 meters wide, and spots are 60 meters apart.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

GEDI data help with forest inventories and with resilience to hazards such as wildfires, floods, hurricanes and landslides.

Source: University of Maryland, GEDI mission overview

For Nature

Measurements of forest height and structure support wildlife habitat conservation.

Source: University of Maryland, GEDI mission overview
