Veggie Space Garden (NASA)

NASA's Veggie garden grows fresh food on the International Space Station; in 2015 astronauts ate the first lettuce grown in it (NASA).

Image: NASA/KSC.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 1 source cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • Astronauts live on the space station for months.
  • Veggie is a little garden with special lights that grows lettuce in space.
  • In 2015 astronauts ate a salad they grew there.

The Five W's of Veggie Space Garden (NASA)

Who

NASA and astronauts aboard the International Space Station.

Source: NASA, Veggie fact sheet

What

A small plant-growth unit with LED lights and seed "pillows" that grows leafy greens in microgravity.

Source: NASA, Veggie fact sheet

When

In August 2015, astronauts harvested and ate red romaine lettuce grown in Veggie.

Source: NASA, Veggie fact sheet

Where

The International Space Station.

Source: NASA, Veggie fact sheet

Why

Long trips to the Moon and Mars will need fresh food grown along the way.

Source: NASA, Veggie fact sheet

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

Veggie showed that astronauts can safely grow and eat fresh crops in space, a step toward feeding crews far from Earth.

Source: NASA, Veggie fact sheet

For Further Information: NASA, Veggie fact sheet

In 2015, astronauts on the space station ate lettuce they grew themselves.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Fresh plants give astronauts nutrition and a mood boost on long missions.

Source: NASA, Veggie fact sheet

For Nature

Lessons from growing food with little water and energy in space can help indoor farming on Earth.

Source: NASA, Veggie fact sheet
