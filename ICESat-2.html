ICESat-2 (NASA)

NASA's ICESat-2 laser fires 10,000 pulses a second to measure ice sheets, sea ice, forests and coastal seafloor in 3D.

Image: NASA/Bill Ingalls.

ICESat-2: Mission Status

Active. Dates per NASA; missions shift.

The Five W's of ICESat-2

Who

NASA.

Source: NASA, ICESat-2

What

A satellite laser altimeter measuring surface height in 3D.

Source: NASA, ICESat-2

When

Launched September 15, 2018 from Vandenberg; operating.

Source: NASA, ICESat-2

Where

Polar orbit, covering ice sheets, sea ice, forests and coasts.

Source: NASA, ICESat-2

Why

To measure how much and why Earth's ice and surface are changing.

Source: NASA, ICESat-2

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

NASA's ICESat-2 launched on September 15, 2018 and carries a photon-counting laser altimeter that measures the elevation of ice sheets, glaciers, sea ice, tree canopies and the ocean in 3D detail. The laser pulses 10,000 times a second; each pulse releases about 300 trillion photons, and only about a dozen return. It helps scientists see why, and how much, Earth is changing in a warming climate.

Source: NASA, ICESat-2

New ICESat-2 data even map shallow near-shore seafloor from space.
