Justdiggit

With farmers and pastoralists in Africa, Justdiggit is restoring 525,000 hectares of dry land and has brought back 32.3 million trees (organization figures).

Image: NASA (STS072-722-004).

  1. Home
  2. Forests
  3. Justdiggit

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 1 source cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • In some dry places, rain runs off the hard ground instead of soaking in.
  • Justdiggit helps people dig little smile-shaped holes that catch the rain.
  • Then grass and trees grow back and the land turns green.

The Five W's of Justdiggit

Who

Justdiggit, a Dutch nonprofit, working with local partners and millions of farmers and pastoralists.

Source: Justdiggit, Our impact

What

Rainwater harvesting with "Earth Smile" bunds and regrowing trees from old stumps (farmer managed natural regeneration).

Source: Justdiggit, Our impact

When

It brought back 32.3 million trees over seven years and retained 10.5 billion liters of water in 2025.

Source: Justdiggit, Our impact

Where

Dry lands in Africa, including Kenya and Tanzania.

Source: Justdiggit, Our impact

Why

Degraded land loses rain to runoff, and bare soil heats up and erodes.

Source: Justdiggit, Our impact

Impact Statement

Nonprofit solution · active

Justdiggit reports 525,000 hectares under restoration, more than 800,000 water bunds dug and 32.3 million trees recovered (organization figures).

Source: Justdiggit, Our impact

For Further Information: Justdiggit, Our impact

Justdiggit digs smile-shaped pits that catch rain and turn dry land green.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Greener farmland can raise crop yields and income, and 879 Maasai women run 43 grass seed banks (organization figures).

Source: Justdiggit, Our impact

For Nature

Returning vegetation cools the ground, holds water in the soil and brings back insects, birds and wildlife.

Source: Justdiggit, Our impact
