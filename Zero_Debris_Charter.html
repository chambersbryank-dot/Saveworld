Zero Debris Charter (ESA)

The Zero Debris Charter sets shared targets to stop creating space debris by 2030; by May 2026 it had 228 signatories from 34 countries (ESA).

Image: NASA, ISS Expedition 23 crew (iss023e057948).

  1. Home
  2. Space Disposal

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 2 sources cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • Old satellites, rocket parts and broken pieces left in orbit that no longer do anything useful. Glossary is old satellites and broken pieces still flying around Earth.
  • The Zero Debris Charter is a promise to stop making new space junk by 2030.
  • Many space companies and countries signed it so useful satellites can stay safe.

The Five W's of Zero Debris Charter (ESA)

Who

ESA facilitated it; 40 space organizations wrote it, and companies, agencies, universities and countries have signed it.

Source: ESA, The Zero Debris Charter

What

A non-binding charter with guiding principles and measurable targets for preventing and removing space debris.

Source: ESA, The Zero Debris Charter

When

Published in 2023, with the goal of debris-neutral space activity by 2030.

Source: ESA, The Zero Debris Charter

Where

Earth The curved path an object takes as it travels around a planet, moon or star, like a satellite circling Earth. Glossary, with signatories in Europe and beyond.

Source: ESA Space Safety, Zero Debris policy and governance (May 2026)

Why

Dead satellites and broken pieces in orbit can collide and create more debris, putting useful satellites at risk.

Source: ESA, The Zero Debris Charter

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

By May 2026 the charter had 228 signatories from 34 countries, including 21 national governments, and a new coordination group to turn the targets into action.

Source: ESA Space Safety, Zero Debris policy and governance (May 2026)

For Further Information: ESA, The Zero Debris Charter

More than 220 space organizations and countries have signed a charter to stop creating space debris by 2030.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Satellites for weather, navigation and communication serve people every day; keeping orbit clean protects those services.

Source: ESA, The Zero Debris Charter

For Nature

Earth-observation satellites that watch forests, oceans and climate depend on a safe orbit to keep working.

Source: ESA, The Zero Debris Charter
