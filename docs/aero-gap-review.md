# Aerospace gap review

Checked 21 November 2026. This list is topics named on a public university syllabus or curriculum page that Saving Planets does not yet teach as a unit or course. Nothing here has been added to a course page. A school home page that does not publish a topic list is not used to invent a gap.

Our courses in this review: AERO 101, 310, 320, 330, 340, 410, 420, 430, 440, 450, 470, 490, plus the rocket and jet tracks.

## AERO 101 — Introduction to Aerospace Engineering

MIT 16.00 says student teams design, build, and fly radio-controlled lighter-than-air vehicles, with required design reviews before a race. Our course has a design-process unit and a card-stock glider lab. It does not have a lighter-than-air vehicle project or a race.

Source: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-00-introduction-to-aerospace-engineering-and-design-spring-2003/

## AERO 310 — Aerodynamics I

MIT 16.100 says students will meet these models. Ours does not teach them as units:

- 3-D vortex lattice methods
- Thin-airfoil models for supersonic flow
- Shock-expansion theory, transonic drag rise, and wing sweep used to delay drag rise
- Wind-tunnel testing with wall corrections
- Finite-volume approximations to the compressible Euler and Navier-Stokes equations
- A team computational and experimental design project (the 2005 offering used a blended-wing-body aircraft)

Our unit 6 already includes panel methods, and unit 8 already names the Navier-Stokes equations. Those are not listed as gaps.

Source: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-100-aerodynamics-fall-2005/pages/syllabus/

## AERO 320 — Aircraft Performance

No separate performance syllabus was published on the MIT pages checked. Georgia Tech lists AE 3330 Introduction to Aerospace Vehicle Performance, which matches the scope of this course, and also lists AE 2610 Introduction to Experimental Methods in Aerospace and AE 3610 Experiments in Fluid and Solid Mechanics. We do not have an experimental-methods course or a fluids-and-solids laboratory course.

Sources: https://catalog.gatech.edu/programs/aerospace-engineering-bs/ and https://catalog.erau.edu/programs/undergraduate/bachelors/aerospace-engineering/ (AERO 314 and AERO 314L, Experimental Aerodynamics and its laboratory)

## AERO 330 — Aerospace Structures

The MIT 16.20 HTML syllabus does not list lecture topics. It points to a PDF of Unified Engineering structures topics. No topic gap is claimed from that HTML page.

Georgia Tech lists COE 3001 Mechanics of Deformable Bodies and AE 3140 Structural Analysis, which match our structures and mechanics-of-materials scope, and AE 3610 Experiments in Fluid and Solid Mechanics, which we do not have.

Source: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-20-structural-mechanics-fall-2002/pages/syllabus/ and https://catalog.gatech.edu/programs/aerospace-engineering-bs/

## AERO 340 — Orbital Mechanics

MIT 16.346 names these topics. Ours does not teach them as units:

- Lambert’s problem
- Orbit determination
- Multi-body methods
- Recursive algorithms for space navigation

Our units already cover the two-body problem, Kepler’s equation, transfers, and interplanetary patched conics.

Source: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-346-astrodynamics-fall-2008/pages/syllabus/

## AERO 410 — Propulsion

MIT 16.50’s calendar names these sessions. A search of our propulsion course does not turn them up as units:

- Solid-propellant gas generators, stability, and grain design
- Nozzle flow of reacting gases, and temperature-dependent specific heats
- Rocket casing structural design
- Ablative cooling
- Thrust vectoring
- Compressor performance maps, compressor blading, and multi-staging
- Turbine solidity, blade temperature, and turbine cooling
- Aircraft engine noise
- Rotordynamics of the jet engine

MIT 16.512 says it adds depth on chemical rockets beyond 16.50, and that a companion course, 16.522, covers electric rockets and small chemical rockets for satellites. Our unit 9 introduces electric propulsion. It is not a 16.522-depth treatment. The 16.512 HTML syllabus does not name further subtopics.

Sources: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-50-introduction-to-propulsion-systems-spring-2012/pages/syllabus/ and https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-512-rocket-propulsion-fall-2005/pages/syllabus/

## AERO 420 — Flight Dynamics and Controls

MIT 16.333’s lecture table includes Modeling and System ID, and a Lateral Autopilot block. We have a pitch-attitude autopilot and state-space form of the equations of motion. We do not have system identification from flight data, or a lateral autopilot.

Source: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-333-aircraft-stability-and-control-fall-2004/pages/syllabus/

## AERO 430 — Spacecraft Design

MIT 16.851 says students select launch systems and see how the launcher affects the satellite, and each student uses Satellite Tool Kit. Our course sizes orbits, power, and links. It does not teach launch-vehicle selection or Satellite Tool Kit, and it does not have students code subsystem software modules.

Source: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-851-satellite-engineering-fall-2003/pages/syllabus/

## AERO 440 — Heat Transfer

MIT 2.51 says the topics include mass transfer at low rates and evaporation, spectral and solar radiation, and numerical simulation of conduction. Our course introduces boiling and condensation and mentions a numerical method when the lumped model fails. It does not teach mass transfer, spectral or solar radiation, or a numerical conduction method.

Source: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-51-intermediate-heat-and-mass-transfer-fall-2008/pages/syllabus/

## AERO 450 — Avionics and Systems engineering

No MIT 16.842 topic list was opened for this pass. Georgia Tech lists AE 3530 System Dynamics and Vibration and AE 4610 Dynamics and Control Laboratory. We do not have a vibration course or a dynamics-and-control laboratory course.

Source: https://catalog.gatech.edu/programs/aerospace-engineering-bs/

## AERO 470 — Sustainable Aviation

MIT 16.885J is linked for its environmental-factors lecture. A topic-by-topic syllabus for that lecture was not on the lecture-notes index used here, so no extra topic is claimed from it.

Georgia Tech’s degree still requires the experimental and communications courses named under AERO 320. Those are track gaps, not extra climate topics.

## AERO 490 — Capstone

Purdue says every student completes a team senior design that leads to a preliminary design of an aircraft or a spacecraft. Our capstone is one self-study course with aircraft and CubeSat tracks. It is not a team build.

Georgia Tech offers a rotorcraft design pair, AE 4331 and AE 4332, beside aircraft and space design. We do not have a rotorcraft design course.

Embry-Riddle lists a year-long aircraft design pair, AERO 420 and AERO 421. Our capstone is one course.

Sources: https://engineering.purdue.edu/AAE/academics/undergraduate and https://catalog.gatech.edu/programs/aerospace-engineering-bs/ and https://catalog.erau.edu/programs/undergraduate/bachelors/aerospace-engineering/

## Rocket and jet tracks

Shared gaps, from the degree pages above, that the tracks do not yet offer as courses:

- Experimental aerodynamics with a laboratory (Embry-Riddle AERO 314 and AERO 314L; Georgia Tech AE 2610 and AE 3610)
- Technical communication and speech (Embry-Riddle COMM 219 Speech; Georgia Tech AE 2611 Technical Communications). The jet track already marks technical writing as a module without a course page.
- Rotorcraft design (Georgia Tech AE 4331 and AE 4332). Aero Math teaches rotor momentum theory. That is not a design course.
- A team senior design (Purdue). Our capstone is self-study.

Purdue also names Autonomy and Control as a senior specialization. We have avionics and flight dynamics. We do not have an autonomy course.

Stanford’s published course list (vector calculus through aircraft and spacecraft design, including mechanics of composite materials) matches topics we already teach at course-name level. No finer gap is claimed from that page.

Source: https://aa.stanford.edu/academics-admissions/undergraduate-program
