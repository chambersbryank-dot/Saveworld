Earth Observation

Impact Statement

Seeing the Whole Planet

You can't protect what you can't measure. These satellites map moving ground, forest carbon, methane leaks and the ozone over the whole planet, and much of their data is free for anyone to use.

The Five W's of Earth Observation

Who

NASA, ISRO, ESA and NOAA, plus Carbon Mapper and Planet.

What

Satellites that map moving ground, forest carbon, methane leaks and the whole sunlit Earth.

When

Ongoing; each mission section below gives its own dates and sources.

Where

Low Earth orbit and the Sun-Earth L1 point (DSCOVR).

Why

You can't protect what you can't measure, and much of this data is free for anyone to use.

NISAR (NASA–ISRO)

Agency solution · active

NISAR launched from India on July 30, 2025. Its two radars (NASA's L-band and ISRO's S-band) see through clouds and darkness, scanning nearly all of Earth's land and ice surfaces twice every 12 days.

Source: NASA, NISAR launch

Source: NASA, NISAR quick facts

By comparing passes, it can detect ground or ice moving by about a centimeter, useful for earthquakes, landslides, sinking ground, glaciers and crops.

Source: NASA, NISAR quick facts

Biomass (ESA)

Agency solution · active

ESA's Biomass satellite launched on April 29, 2025 on Vega-C. It carries the first P-band synthetic aperture radar in space; its long wavelength (about 70 cm) reaches through forest canopies to measure the woody trunks and branches where carbon is stored.

Source: ESA, Biomass launched to count forest carbon

Its maps of forest carbon, height and disturbance give the world a better count of deforestation and regrowth. See our Forests hub for the groups replanting on the ground.

Tanager-1 (Carbon Mapper / Planet)

Corporate / nonprofit solution · active

Tanager-1 was built by Planet with a NASA JPL-designed imaging spectrometer for the nonprofit Carbon Mapper coalition. Its first published methane detection, on September 19, 2024, was a landfill plume in Karachi, Pakistan, with a preliminary estimated rate of 1,200 kg of methane per hour (Carbon Mapper estimate, reported by NASA).

Source: NASA, First greenhouse gas plumes detected

Source: Carbon Mapper, first Tanager-1 detections (company release)

Pinpointing single leaks lets operators and regulators fix them fast, one of the quickest ways to cut warming.

DSCOVR / EPIC (NASA–NOAA)

Agency solution · active

NASA's EPIC camera rides on NOAA's DSCOVR spacecraft at the L1 point, photographing the entire sunlit face of Earth several times a day, with an archive back to 2015.

Source: NASA, EPIC

On October 5, 2026, NASA released a new high-resolution EPIC tropospheric ozone product, gridded at 0.25°. This is the honest home for "space watches ozone": EPIC measures it, alongside the ground-based measurements that first found the ozone hole.

Source: NASA Earthdata, EPIC tropospheric ozone release

SWOT (NASA–CNES)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

SWOT (Surface Water and Ocean Topography) is the first global survey of Earth's fresh water. It measures more than 95% of lakes larger than 15 acres and rivers wider than 330 feet, tracking how much water flows through rivers and is stored in lakes and reservoirs.

Source: NASA JPL, SWOT freshwater survey

Source: NASA Earthdata, SWOT data release

Value to Earth: water managers can see droughts and floods building. Value to the atmosphere and climate: SWOT sees ocean eddies smaller than 60 miles across, which move heat and carbon between the ocean and the air. Value to space: it shows what a radar satellite in orbit can measure that no ground network can reach.

Source: NASA JPL, SWOT freshwater survey

For Further Information: NASA JPL, SWOT freshwater survey

NASA–CNES SWOT keeps watch on the water levels of more than a million lakes larger than 15 acres.

The Five W's of SWOT

Who

NASA and the French space agency CNES (a U.S.-European mission).

Source: NASA JPL, SWOT freshwater survey

What

A radar interferometer satellite that measures the height of water on land and at sea.

Source: NASA JPL, SWOT freshwater survey

Source: NASA Earthdata, SWOT data release

When

Operating now; it covers the whole planet every 21 days.

Source: NASA Earthdata, SWOT data release

Where

Orbiting 554 miles (891 km) up, scanning between 78° North and 78° South.

Source: NASA Earthdata, SWOT data release

Why

Fresh water is unevenly measured on the ground; SWOT gives every country the same view of its rivers and lakes.

Source: NASA JPL, SWOT freshwater survey

PACE (NASA)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

PACE (Plankton, Aerosol, Cloud, ocean Ecosystem) launched on February 8, 2024. Its hyperspectral Ocean Color Instrument tracks phytoplankton, tiny organisms that start the marine food web and drive the ocean's carbon cycle, and it shows how aerosols may feed their growth.

Source: NASA, PACE launch

Source: NASA, PACE mission

For Further Information: NASA, PACE mission

The Five W's of PACE

Who

NASA, built and operated by Goddard Space Flight Center.

Source: NASA, PACE mission

What

A satellite whose hyperspectral Ocean Color Instrument measures light from ultraviolet to near-infrared.

Source: NASA, PACE launch

When

Launched February 8, 2024 on a Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral.

Source: NASA, PACE launch

Where

Orbiting 676 km (420 mi) above Earth, circling the planet about every 98.3 minutes.

Source: NASA, PACE mission

Why

Phytoplankton feed ocean life and help move carbon; tracking them shows how the ocean and climate are changing.

Source: NASA, PACE mission

Copernicus Sentinel-6B (ESA / NASA)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

Sea level rise is one of the clearest measures of a warming planet. Sentinel-6B launched on November 17, 2025 to continue the reference record of global sea level from space that began in the early 1990s, measuring sea-surface height with a Poseidon-4 radar altimeter and a microwave radiometer. Together with Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich, the mission is planned to run until at least 2030.

Source: ESA, Copernicus Sentinel-6

For Further Information: ESA, Copernicus Sentinel-6

The Five W's of Copernicus Sentinel-6B

Who

ESA and the European Commission's Copernicus programme with EUMETSAT, NASA and NOAA.

Source: ESA, Copernicus Sentinel-6

What

A radar altimetry satellite that measures the height of the sea surface.

Source: ESA, Copernicus Sentinel-6

When

Launched November 17, 2025 from Vandenberg, California; first images released December 2025.

Source: ESA, Copernicus Sentinel-6

Where

Low Earth orbit, measuring about 95% of the ocean.

Source: ESA, Copernicus Sentinel-6

Why

An unbroken sea-level record helps coastal cities plan for rising water.

Source: ESA, Copernicus Sentinel-6

GOSAT-GW "IBUKI GW" (JAXA)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

JAXA's IBUKI GW launched on June 29, 2025 on H-IIA No. 50. Its TANSO-3 instrument measures the greenhouse gases carbon dioxide and methane, plus nitrogen dioxide, while AMSR3 observes the water cycle, putting greenhouse-gas and water-cycle monitoring on one satellite.

Source: JAXA, IBUKI GW (GOSAT-GW) status

Source: JAXA, IBUKI GW project page

For Further Information: JAXA, IBUKI GW project page

Who

JAXA with Japan's Ministry of the Environment and the National Institute for Environmental Studies.

Source: JAXA, IBUKI GW (GOSAT-GW) status

What

A satellite carrying TANSO-3 for greenhouse gases and AMSR3 for the water cycle.

Source: JAXA, IBUKI GW project page

When

Launched June 29, 2025 from Tanegashima Space Center.

Source: JAXA, IBUKI GW (GOSAT-GW) status

Where

Earth orbit, observing the whole globe.

Source: JAXA, IBUKI GW project page

Why

Countries need independent, consistent measurements of emissions to check climate pledges.

Source: JAXA, IBUKI GW project page

EarthCARE (ESA–JAXA)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

Clouds and aerosols are among the hardest parts of the climate to measure. EarthCARE launched on May 29, 2024 with four instruments that measure them together. Its JAXA Cloud Profiling Radar is the first spaceborne cloud radar with Doppler capability, measuring the vertical motion of cloud particles from orbit.

Source: ESA, EarthCARE

Source: JAXA, EarthCARE Cloud Profiling Radar

For Further Information: ESA, EarthCARE

EarthCARE carries the first cloud radar in space that can measure how fast cloud particles move up and down.

The Five W's of EarthCARE

Who

ESA and JAXA; JAXA and NICT built the Cloud Profiling Radar.

Source: ESA, EarthCARE

Source: JAXA, EarthCARE Cloud Profiling Radar

What

A satellite with four instruments, including JAXA's Doppler Cloud Profiling Radar.

Source: ESA, EarthCARE

When

Launched May 29, 2024 from Vandenberg, California on a Falcon 9.

Source: ESA, EarthCARE

Where

Low Earth orbit, profiling the atmosphere beneath it.

Source: ESA, EarthCARE

Why

Knowing whether clouds warm or cool the planet sharpens climate forecasts.

Source: ESA, EarthCARE

ALOS-4 "DAICHI-4" (JAXA)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

Tropical forests are often under cloud, which hides clearing from ordinary cameras. JAXA's ALOS-4 "DAICHI-4", launched July 1, 2024, carries the L-band PALSAR-3 radar, which sees through clouds and widens the observation swath from 50 km to 200 km at high resolution. Its predecessor's data already powers JAXA's JJ-FAST system, which detects deforestation of a few hectares in 77 countries.

Source: JAXA, ALOS-4 launch result

Source: JAXA, ALOS-4 project page

For Further Information: JAXA, ALOS-4 project page

The Five W's of ALOS-4 "DAICHI-4"

Who

JAXA, the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency.

Source: JAXA, ALOS-4 project page

What

A radar satellite with the PALSAR-3 L-band synthetic aperture radar and a ship-tracking receiver.

Source: JAXA, ALOS-4 project page

When

Launched July 1, 2024 on H3 flight No. 3 from Tanegashima.

Source: JAXA, ALOS-4 launch result

Where

Low Earth orbit, imaging land, forests and oceans day and night.

Source: JAXA, ALOS-4 project page

Why

Forests cover 30% of the world's land, and deforestation is strongly linked to rising greenhouse gases.

Source: JAXA, ALOS-4 project page

GRACE-FO (NASA / Germany)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

GRACE-FO, the US-German successor to GRACE (2002-2017), launched on May 22, 2018 and tracks Earth's water movement by measuring tiny changes in gravity. Its data reveal groundwater loss, ice sheet change and sea level rise; scientists using these satellites found Earth's total freshwater dropped abruptly starting in May 2014.

Source: NASA, GRACE-FO

Source: NASA JPL, GRACE-FO

ICESat-2 (NASA)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

NASA's ICESat-2 launched on September 15, 2018 and carries a photon-counting laser altimeter that measures the elevation of ice sheets, glaciers, sea ice, tree canopies and the ocean in 3D detail. The laser pulses 10,000 times a second; each pulse releases about 300 trillion photons, and only about a dozen return. It helps scientists see why, and how much, Earth is changing in a warming climate.

Source: NASA, ICESat-2

New ICESat-2 data even map shallow near-shore seafloor from space.

For Further Information: NASA, ICESat-2

ICESat-2's laser fires 10,000 pulses a second, each with about 300 trillion photons, and only about a dozen return to the satellite.

Copernicus Sentinel-1 (ESA)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

The Copernicus Sentinel-1 satellites carry an advanced radar that supplies all-weather, day-and-night imagery of Earth's surface. Sentinel-1C launched on December 5, 2024 and Sentinel-1D on November 4, 2025, renewing the fleet. Recent ESA stories show it measuring ground movement after earthquakes and major ice loss from Greenland glaciers.

Source: ESA, Sentinel-1

Radar that ignores weather is vital when storms and floods hit.

For Further Information: ESA, Sentinel-1

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech (contains Copernicus Sentinel-1 data, ESA).

FLEX and Sentinel-3C (ESA)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

ESA's Fluorescence Explorer (FLEX) and Copernicus Sentinel-3C launched together on a Vega-C on September 15, 2026. FLEX measures the faint fluorescence plants give off during photosynthesis to reveal the health of the world's vegetation and how carbon moves between plants and the air. Sentinel-3C, which returned its first views of Earth in October 2026, continues the Sentinel-3 record of ocean and land.

Its Ocean Color Monitor, Sea Surface Temperature Monitor and Ku-band scatterometer keep ocean color and wind data flowing for operational use; early images captured Cyclone Mandous and an algal bloom.
