Earth Observation

NASA Blue Marble

Impact Statement

Seeing the Whole Planet

You can't protect what you can't measure. These satellites map moving ground, forest carbon, methane leaks and the ozone over the whole planet, and much of their data is free for anyone to use.

The Five W's of Earth Observation

Who

NASA, ISRO, ESA and NOAA, plus Carbon Mapper and Planet.

What

Satellites that map moving ground, forest carbon, methane leaks and the whole sunlit Earth.

When

Ongoing; each mission section below gives its own dates and sources.

Where

Low Earth orbit and the Sun-Earth L1 point (DSCOVR).

Why

You can't protect what you can't measure, and much of this data is free for anyone to use.

Missions and Solutions

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS)

Free global forecasts of air pollution, smoke and dust, made twice a day; more than 23,500 users (CAMS, 2024).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

GSMaP Global Rainfall Map (JAXA)

Free hourly global rainfall maps from a constellation of satellites; 18,673 registered users in 163 countries and regions (JAXA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Joint Polar Satellite System (NOAA)

Polar weather satellites behind forecasts and hurricane warnings; polar orbiters supply about 85% of weather model data (NOAA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

RADARSAT Constellation Mission (Canada)

Three radar satellites that see through cloud, watching sea ice, ships and floods; free public images (Canadian Space Agency).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

The GLOBE Program

Students and citizen scientists collect environmental data for science; 974,936 GLOBE citizen scientists in 127 countries (GLOBE figures).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Methane Alert and Response System (UNEP)

UN satellite alerts tell governments and companies about huge methane leaks; 25 cases of action in 10 countries since 2022 (UNEP).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

FIRMS Fire Information for Resource Management System (NASA)

Free near-real-time fire maps and email alerts from NASA satellites; global data within 3 hours (NASA FIRMS).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus Emergency Management Service: On-Demand Mapping

EU satellite mapping for disasters, 24/7 since 2012; more than 1,000 activations reaching over 120 countries (Copernicus EMS).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

International Charter: Space and Major Disasters

Space agencies share free satellite images with rescue teams after major disasters; 1,000 activations in 146 countries (International Charter).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Libera Earth Energy Instrument (NASA)

A new instrument that will measure the energy Earth sends back to space; set to fly on JPSS-4 in 2027 (NASA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus Sentinel-3 (ESA / EUMETSAT)

Measures ocean color, sea level, temperatures, ice and wildfires; Sentinel-3C joined the fleet in September 2026 (ESA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

HydroGNSS (ESA)

Two small satellites that read reflected GPS-style signals to measure soil moisture and wetlands; data free worldwide since July 2026 (ESA).

Active🧊 Polar

PREFIRE Polar CubeSats (NASA)

Two tiny CubeSats measuring how much heat the Arctic and Antarctica release to space; extended through at least September 2026 (NASA JPL).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus Sentinel-5P (ESA)

The first Copernicus mission dedicated to the atmosphere; maps air pollution and methane worldwide every day (ESA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

OCO-2 Orbiting Carbon Observatory (NASA)

Measures carbon dioxide around the planet to find carbon sources and sinks; launched in 2014 and still working (NASA JPL).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

EMIT (NASA)

An imaging spectrometer on the space station that maps desert dust minerals and spots large methane leaks (NASA JPL).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

FireSat (Earth Fire Alliance)

New FireSat satellites can spot wildfires as small as 5 by 5 meters; the first three reached orbit in July 2026 (Earth Fire Alliance).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus CO2M Carbon Dioxide Monitoring (ESA / EU)

Three planned satellites will measure the carbon dioxide and methane people release, so countries can check their emission cuts; first launch planned for 2027 (ESA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

The Science of Tsunami Detection

Deep-ocean buoys, satellites, GPS signals and seafloor cables spot tsunamis in the open ocean so coastal communities get time to reach high ground (NOAA, NASA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

SARSAT Search and Rescue Satellites (NOAA)

NOAA's SARSAT satellites relay distress beacon signals to rescuers; the international system has supported more than 50,000 rescues worldwide since 1982 (NOAA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus Sentinel-4 (ESA / EUMETSAT)

From geostationary orbit on MTG-S1, Sentinel-4 measures air pollution over Europe and North Africa every hour (ESA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

TEMPO Air Quality Mission (NASA)

From geostationary orbit, NASA's TEMPO measures air pollution over North America every daylight hour, a first from space (NASA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

NISAR (NASA–ISRO)

NISAR launched from India on July 30, 2025.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Biomass (ESA)

ESA's Biomass satellite launched on April 29, 2025 on Vega-C.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Tanager-1 (Carbon Mapper / Planet)

Tanager-1 was built by Planet with a NASA JPL-designed imaging spectrometer for the nonprofit Carbon Mapper coalition.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

DSCOVR / EPIC (NASA–NOAA)

NASA's EPIC camera rides on NOAA's DSCOVR spacecraft at the L1 point, photographing the entire sunlit face of Earth several times a day, with an archive back to 2015.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

SWOT (NASA–CNES)

SWOT measures more than 95% of Earth's lakes larger than 15 acres and rivers wider than 330 feet, the first global survey of fresh water.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

PACE (NASA)

NASA's PACE reads the color of the ocean to track phytoplankton, the base of the marine food web, plus aerosols and clouds.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus Sentinel-6B (ESA / NASA)

Sentinel-6B, launched November 17, 2025, continues the precise record of global sea level that began in the early 1990s.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

GOSAT-GW "IBUKI GW" (JAXA)

JAXA's IBUKI GW tracks carbon dioxide, methane and nitrogen dioxide while also measuring the water cycle.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

EarthCARE (ESA–JAXA)

ESA–JAXA EarthCARE carries the first space cloud radar that can measure how cloud particles move up and down.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

ALOS-4 "DAICHI-4" (JAXA)

JAXA's DAICHI-4 radar sees through clouds over a 200 km swath, continuing the radar record used for deforestation alerts.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

GRACE-FO (NASA / Germany)

Twin US-German satellites weigh Earth's water by measuring gravity, tracking groundwater, ice and sea level since 2018.

Active🧊 Polar

ICESat-2 (NASA)

NASA's ICESat-2 laser fires 10,000 pulses a second to measure ice sheets, sea ice, forests and coastal seafloor in 3D.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus Sentinel-1 (ESA)

Sentinel-1 radar sees through clouds, day and night, to map floods, earthquakes and ice; Sentinel-1C and 1D joined in 2024 and 2025.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

FLEX and Sentinel-3C (ESA)

Launched together on September 15, 2026, ESA's FLEX maps the faint glow of photosynthesis while Sentinel-3C watches oceans and land.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

EOS-06 / Oceansat-3 (ISRO)

India's EOS-06, the third Oceansat, tracks ocean color, sea-surface temperature and winds; it imaged Cyclone Mandous and an algal bloom on day one.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus Sentinel-2C (ESA)

Sentinel-2C joined the Copernicus fleet in September 2024, imaging land and coasts in 13 bands so forests and crops are checked every five days.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

MetOp-SG-A1 and Sentinel-5A (ESA / EUMETSAT)

Launched August 2025 on Ariane 6, MetOp-SG-A1 carries Sentinel-5, which delivers daily global data on air pollutants, ozone and climate gases.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Landsat 9 (NASA / USGS)

Landsat 9 continues a 50-year record of Earth’s land, collecting 740 scenes a day and, with Landsat 8, seeing each place every 8 days.

Active🧊 Polar

CryoSat Ice Mission (ESA)

CryoSat measures polar sea ice and the great ice sheets from space, and its data revealed 85 hidden lakes under Antarctica (ESA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Meteosat Third Generation (ESA / EUMETSAT)

Three weather satellites now watch Europe and Africa from 36,000 km up, with new images every 2.5 minutes and a camera that catches lightning day and night (ESA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

SMAP Soil Moisture Active Passive (NASA)

SMAP maps the water in Earth's soil from space, helping improve predictions of weather, floods and droughts (NASA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

GCOM-C "SHIKISAI" (JAXA)

Japan's SHIKISAI satellite checks the health of Earth from space every 2 or 3 days, tracking clouds, aerosols, ocean color, plants, snow and ice (JAXA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

FORUM Far-Infrared Mission (ESA)

ESA's FORUM will measure Earth's outgoing far-infrared radiation, never measured from space before, filling a missing piece of the climate jigsaw (ESA).

Active🧊 Polar

CIMR Copernicus Imaging Microwave Radiometer (ESA)

Two Copernicus satellites will measure sea ice, sea-surface temperature and salinity over 95% of the globe every day, with sub-daily polar views (ESA).

Active🧊 Polar

CRISTAL Polar Ice and Snow Altimeter (ESA)

CRISTAL will carry the first dual-frequency radar altimeter to measure polar sea-ice thickness and the snow on top, following ESA's CryoSat (ESA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

CHIME Hyperspectral Imaging Mission (ESA)

CHIME will image Earth in more than 200 colours of light to support sustainable farming, food security and biodiversity (ESA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

GeoXO Geostationary Extended Observations (NOAA / NASA)

NOAA's next generation of geostationary weather satellites, planned from 2032, will improve warnings of severe weather and hazards (NOAA).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

INSAT-3DS Weather Satellite (ISRO)

India's INSAT-3DS watches weather, land and oceans from geostationary orbit and carries a search-and-rescue transponder (ISRO).

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Galileo Satellite Navigation (EU / ESA)

Europe's Galileo is the world's most precise satellite navigation system, serving over five billion smartphone users and helping rescues (ESA).
