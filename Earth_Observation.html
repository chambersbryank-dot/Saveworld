Earth Observation

Impact Statement

Seeing the Whole Planet

You can't protect what you can't measure. These satellites map moving ground, forest carbon, methane leaks and the ozone over the whole planet, and much of their data is free for anyone to use.

The Five W's of Earth Observation

Who

NASA, ISRO, ESA and NOAA, plus Carbon Mapper and Planet.

What

Satellites that map moving ground, forest carbon, methane leaks and the whole sunlit Earth.

When

Ongoing; each mission section below gives its own dates and sources.

Where

Low Earth orbit and the Sun-Earth L1 point (DSCOVR).

Why

You can't protect what you can't measure, and much of this data is free for anyone to use.

Missions and Solutions

Active🌍 Earth Observation

NISAR (NASA–ISRO)

NISAR launched from India on July 30, 2025.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Biomass (ESA)

ESA's Biomass satellite launched on April 29, 2025 on Vega-C.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Tanager-1 (Carbon Mapper / Planet)

Tanager-1 was built by Planet with a NASA JPL-designed imaging spectrometer for the nonprofit Carbon Mapper coalition.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

DSCOVR / EPIC (NASA–NOAA)

NASA's EPIC camera rides on NOAA's DSCOVR spacecraft at the L1 point, photographing the entire sunlit face of Earth several times a day, with an archive back to 2015.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

SWOT (NASA–CNES)

SWOT measures more than 95% of Earth's lakes larger than 15 acres and rivers wider than 330 feet, the first global survey of fresh water.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

PACE (NASA)

NASA's PACE reads the color of the ocean to track phytoplankton, the base of the marine food web, plus aerosols and clouds.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus Sentinel-6B (ESA / NASA)

Sentinel-6B, launched November 17, 2025, continues the precise record of global sea level that began in the early 1990s.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

GOSAT-GW "IBUKI GW" (JAXA)

JAXA's IBUKI GW tracks carbon dioxide, methane and nitrogen dioxide while also measuring the water cycle.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

EarthCARE (ESA–JAXA)

ESA–JAXA EarthCARE carries the first space cloud radar that can measure how cloud particles move up and down.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

ALOS-4 "DAICHI-4" (JAXA)

JAXA's DAICHI-4 radar sees through clouds over a 200 km swath, continuing the radar record used for deforestation alerts.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

GRACE-FO (NASA / Germany)

Twin US-German satellites weigh Earth's water by measuring gravity, tracking groundwater, ice and sea level since 2018.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

ICESat-2 (NASA)

NASA's ICESat-2 laser fires 10,000 pulses a second to measure ice sheets, sea ice, forests and coastal seafloor in 3D.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

Copernicus Sentinel-1 (ESA)

Sentinel-1 radar sees through clouds, day and night, to map floods, earthquakes and ice; Sentinel-1C and 1D joined in 2024 and 2025.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

FLEX and Sentinel-3C (ESA)

Launched together on September 15, 2026, ESA's FLEX maps the faint glow of photosynthesis while Sentinel-3C watches oceans and land.

Active🌍 Earth Observation

EOS-06 / Oceansat-3 (ISRO)

India's EOS-06, the third Oceansat, tracks ocean color, sea-surface temperature and winds; it imaged Cyclone Mandous and an algal bloom on day one.
