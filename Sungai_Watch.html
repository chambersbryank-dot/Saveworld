Sungai Watch

Trash barriers and daily cleanups stopping plastic in Indonesia's rivers.

The Five W's of Sungai Watch

Who

Sungai Watch, founded in 2020 in Bali by siblings Gary, Kelly, and Sam Bencheghib. A registered charity in Indonesia and a 501(c)(3) in the U.S., with a team of 180-plus River Warriors.

What

Installing simple, custom-fitted trash barriers in rivers, then running daily cleanups, sorting every piece into 30-plus material categories, and upcycling what they collect. They also map pollution sources and partner with villages and local governments.

When

Active since 2020. Over 400 barriers installed and more than 4 million kilograms of plastic collected, with expansion from Bali into Java underway.

Where

Indonesia's most polluted rivers, primarily in Bali and East Java — the world's most populated island and home to 90 of the 1,000 most polluted rivers on Earth.

Why

More than 80 percent of ocean plastic starts in rivers. Indonesia ranks second globally in plastic flowing to the sea, and weak waste infrastructure means barriers plus community cleanups are the fastest way to stop it at the source.

Impact Statement

Every dollar funds removal, sorting, and processing that keeps waste out of rivers, landfills, and the sea. They refuse funding from plastic producers, so the work stays independent of the industry creating the problem.
