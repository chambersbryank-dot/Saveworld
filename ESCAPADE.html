ESCAPADE (NASA)

NASA's twin ESCAPADE spacecraft will study how the solar wind strips Mars' atmosphere, after an Earth slingshot in fall 2026.

Image: NASA.

ESCAPADE: Mission Status

Near Earth; NASA plans an Earth slingshot toward Mars in fall 2026 (no exact date published). Dates per NASA; missions shift.

The Five W's of ESCAPADE

Who

NASA, with the mission led by UC Berkeley's Space Sciences Laboratory.

Source: NASA, ESCAPADE

What

Twin orbiters studying Mars' magnetosphere and atmospheric escape.

Source: NASA, ESCAPADE

When

Launched November 13, 2025; Earth slingshot toward Mars in fall 2026.

Source: NASA, ESCAPADE

Where

Mars orbit.

Source: NASA, ESCAPADE

Why

To understand how Mars lost its atmosphere and climate.

Source: NASA, ESCAPADE

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

NASA's ESCAPADE launched on November 13, 2025: two identical spacecraft that will orbit Mars and study how the solar wind interacts with its hybrid magnetosphere and drives atmospheric escape. After a loiter orbit near Earth's Lagrange point 2, they use Earth's gravity in fall 2026 to slingshot toward Mars. The goal is to understand how Mars lost its once-thick atmosphere, one that may have supported liquid water.

Source: NASA, ESCAPADE

Mars shows what happens to a planet that loses its protection; Earth's magnetic field and atmosphere are shields worth understanding.
