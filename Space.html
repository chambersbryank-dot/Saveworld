Computing Above the Clouds

Solar-powered data centers in orbit, zero water cooling, and a new frontier for sustainable tech.

The Five W's of Space Data Centers

Who

Starcloud, formerly Lumen Orbit, launched the first NVIDIA H100 GPU into orbit in November twenty twenty-five and became the fastest unicorn in Y Combinator history. Google's Project Suncatcher put four Tensor Processing Units in space on October first, twenty twenty-six. Cowboy Space, founded by Robinhood co-founder Baiju Bhatt, and startups like Aetherflux and Orbital are racing to build orbital compute constellations.

What

Companies are flying AI chips on satellites to run cloud computing, machine learning inference, and data processing in orbit. The pitch: unlimited solar power above the atmosphere and radiative cooling in the vacuum of space, which eliminates the need for water-based cooling towers that drink billions of gallons on Earth.

When

Starcloud-1 launched November two, twenty twenty-five. Starcloud-2 is planned for twenty twenty-seven, and Starcloud-3, a three-ton two-hundred-kilowatt platform, is gated on commercial Starship around twenty twenty-eight to twenty twenty-nine. Google's Suncatcher test flew October first, twenty twenty-six. Starcloud also signed the first commercial lunar data-center contract with Firefly in September twenty twenty-six.

Where

Low Earth orbit today, with Starcloud filing with the FCC for a constellation of up to eighty-eight thousand satellites. Lunar orbit is next, with a payload targeted for no earlier than twenty twenty-eight aboard Firefly's Elytra vehicle on NASA's Blue Ghost Mission three.

Why

Data centers on Earth consume about seventeen billion gallons of water directly each year in the U.S., and roughly two hundred eleven billion gallons indirectly through electricity generation — about twelve times more. Orbital compute sidesteps both: solar panels in space never set, and heat radiates away into space without a single drop of water.

Impact Statement

U.S. data centers directly consumed about seventeen point four billion gallons of water in twenty twenty-three, while the power plants generating their electricity consumed an estimated two hundred eleven billion gallons — more than twelve times the on-site figure. Google alone used ten point nine billion gallons in twenty twenty-five, up thirty-four percent year over year. Amazon withdrew two point five billion gallons globally that same year.

Space-based data centers offer a path to break that cycle. Continuous sunlight, no evaporative cooling, and no competition with farms or communities for local water supplies. The catch: launch costs still need to fall to around two hundred dollars per kilogram before orbital compute can compete with ground-based infrastructure at scale.
