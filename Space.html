Computing Above the Clouds

Solar-powered data centers in orbit, zero water cooling, and a new frontier for sustainable tech.

The Five W's of Space Data Centers

Who

Starcloud, formerly Lumen Orbit, launched the first NVIDIA H100 GPU into orbit in November 2025 and became the fastest unicorn in Y Combinator history. Google's Project Suncatcher put four Tensor Processing Units in space on October 1, 2026. Cowboy Space, founded by Robinhood co-founder Baiju Bhatt, and startups like Aetherflux and Orbital are racing to build orbital compute constellations.

What

Companies are flying AI chips on satellites to run cloud computing, machine learning inference, and data processing in orbit. The pitch: unlimited solar power above the atmosphere and radiative cooling in the vacuum of space, which eliminates the need for water-based cooling towers that drink billions of gallons on Earth.

When

Starcloud-1 launched November 2, 2025. Starcloud-2 is planned for 2027, and Starcloud-3, a three-ton 200-kilowatt platform, is gated on commercial Starship around 2028 to 2029. Google's Suncatcher test flew October 1, 2026. Starcloud also signed the first commercial lunar data-center contract with Firefly in September 2026.

Where

Low Earth orbit today, with Starcloud filing with the FCC for a constellation of up to 88,000 satellites. Lunar orbit is next, with a payload targeted for no earlier than 2028 aboard Firefly's Elytra vehicle on NASA's Blue Ghost Mission 3.

Why

Data centers on Earth consume about 17 billion gallons of water directly each year in the U.S., and roughly 211 billion gallons indirectly through electricity generation — about twelve times more. Orbital compute sidesteps both: solar panels in space never set, and heat radiates away into space without a single drop of water.

Impact Statement

U.S. data centers directly consumed about 17.4 billion gallons of water in 2023, while the power plants generating their electricity consumed an estimated 211 billion gallons — more than twelve times the on-site figure. Google alone used 10.9 billion gallons in 2025, up 34 percent year over year. Amazon withdrew 2.5 billion gallons globally that same year.

Space-based data centers offer a path to break that cycle. Continuous sunlight, no evaporative cooling, and no competition with farms or communities for local water supplies. The catch: launch costs still need to fall to around $200 per kilogram before orbital compute can compete with ground-based infrastructure at scale.
