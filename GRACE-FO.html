GRACE-FO (NASA / Germany)

Twin US-German satellites weigh Earth's water by measuring gravity, tracking groundwater, ice and sea level since 2018.

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech.

  1. Home

Watch: Satellites That Weigh Earth's Water

Transcript

What you see: SI-generated visuals: twin satellites orbiting Earth, farmland and a drying river, melting ice, Earth from space, then the savingplanets.com end card.

What you hear:

These two satellites can weigh the water on Earth.

As they fly, the distance between them changes by less than the width of a hair, pulled by the gravity of water below.

That shows where groundwater is vanishing, where ice is melting, and where drought is coming.

Saving a planet is saving people.

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

GRACE-FO, the US-German successor to GRACE (2002-2017), launched on May 22, 2018 and tracks Earth's water movement by measuring tiny changes in gravity. Its data reveal groundwater loss, ice sheet change and sea level rise; scientists using these satellites found Earth's total freshwater dropped abruptly starting in May 2014.

Source: NASA, GRACE-FO
