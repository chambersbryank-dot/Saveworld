Rainforest Connection

Solar-powered Guardian listening devices and AI detect chainsaws in real time; Rainforest Connection reports more than 726,000 hectares of rainforest protected (organization figure).

Image: NASA (ISS013-E-74843).

  1. Home
  2. Forests

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 2 sources cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • Rainforest Connection puts listening boxes high up in trees.
  • The boxes use the sun for power and a computer that can tell when it hears a chainsaw.
  • When it hears one, a ranger gets a message on a phone and can go stop the cutting.

The Five W's of Rainforest Connection

Who

Rainforest Connection (RFCx), a nonprofit founded by engineer Topher White.

Source: Rainforest Connection

What

Solar-powered Guardian devices high in trees that listen to the forest and use AI to spot threats.

Source: Rainforest Connection

When

Guardians listen 24/7 and send alerts in real time.

Source: Rainforest Connection

Where

Forests in the Americas, Africa, Asia and Europe, including Brazil, Colombia, Indonesia and Poland.

Source: Rainforest Connection

Why

Illegal logging, poaching and fires are hard to catch in time in huge forests.

Source: Rainforest Connection

Impact Statement

Nonprofit solution · active

When a Guardian hears a chainsaw or gunshot, a nearby ranger gets an app alert with the location and the audio so they can respond before damage is done. RFCx reports more than 726,000 hectares of rainforest protected (organization figure).

Source: Rainforest Connection

For Further Information: Rainforest Connection

Rainforest Connection hides solar-powered listening devices in trees to hear illegal logging as it happens.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Local rangers and Indigenous communities, such as those protecting Brazil's Tembé reserves, get real-time alerts to defend their forests.

Source: Rainforest Connection

For Nature

The same recordings identify thousands of species, helping scientists track wildlife and The variety of all living things in a place: plants, animals, fungi and tiny microbes. Glossary.

Source: Rainforest Connection
