Oceans

Impact Statement

Three groups on this hub pull plastic out of the ocean and the rivers that feed it. The Ocean Cleanup reports about 65 million kg removed from rivers and the ocean.

Organization source: The Ocean Cleanup

4ocean passed 50 million pounds (about 22.7 million kg) removed in February 2026.

Company source: 4ocean, 50 million pounds removed

Plastic Bank reports about 196 million kg of plastic recovered through its collector network.

Company source: Plastic Bank newsletter, June 2026

Together that is more than 280 million kg of plastic kept out of the sea.

The Five W's of Ocean Solutions

Who

The Ocean Cleanup (nonprofit), 4ocean (company) and Plastic Bank (social enterprise).

What

Removing plastic from the open ocean, coastlines and rivers, and paying people to collect it before it reaches the sea.

When

Ongoing. 4ocean passed 50 million pounds removed in February 2026.

Company source: 4ocean, 50 million pounds removed

Where

Ocean garbage patches, coastlines and the rivers that carry plastic to the sea.

Why

Plastic in the ocean breaks into smaller pieces that are far harder to recover. Removing it early, or stopping it upstream, keeps it out of food chains.

Missions and Solutions

Project Seagrass

A UK charity restoring underwater seagrass meadows since 2013; more than 150,000 seeds planted (organization figures).

Coral Gardeners

Grows corals in nurseries and replants them on reefs in French Polynesia, Fiji and Thailand; 238,250 corals planted (organization figures).

The Science of Tsunami Detection

Deep-ocean buoys, satellites, GPS signals and seafloor cables spot tsunamis in the open ocean so coastal communities get time to reach high ground (NOAA, NASA).

Whales, Ocean Gardeners

Whale waste carries nutrients to the ocean surface and fertilizes phytoplankton, the tiny plants at the base of the ocean food web (NOAA Fisheries).

Posidonia Seagrass Meadows

The Mediterranean seagrass Posidonia oceanica forms meadows that are thousands of years old and store carbon in the seafloor (CEAB-CSIC).

Coral IVF (Great Barrier Reef Foundation)

Researchers capture coral spawn and rear millions of baby corals for damaged reefs; the first Coral IVF corals reproduced on the Great Barrier Reef in 2021 (GBRF).

Global Fishing Watch

Global Fishing Watch uses satellites and AI to map fishing; its 2024 Nature study found about 75% of industrial fishing vessels are not publicly tracked (Global Fishing Watch).

Active🌊 Ocean

4ocean

4ocean removes plastic from oceans, rivers, and coastlines with full-time cleanup crews, funded by products made from recovered plastic.

Active🌊 Ocean

The Ocean Cleanup

The Ocean Cleanup removes plastic from oceans and intercepts it in rivers.

Active🌊 Ocean

Plastic Bank

Plastic Bank turns ocean-bound plastic into income for collectors in vulnerable communities.

Active🌊 Ocean

Phytoplankton, the Ocean's Oxygen Engine

Tiny ocean plants produce about half of Earth's oxygen, making phytoplankton one of the planet's most important natural systems.

Active🌊 Ocean

Coral Restoration Foundation

Coral Restoration Foundation had returned 253,833 corals to Florida's reefs by the end of 2024 (organization figure).

Active🌊 Ocean

Kelp Forests

Kelp can grow up to 18 inches a day, building underwater forests that feed and shelter thousands of species.

Active🌊 Ocean

Seagrass Meadows

Seagrass covers just 0.1% of the seafloor but stores 11% of the ocean's buried organic carbon. See also Mangroves on Deforestation.

Active🌊 Ocean

Billion Oyster Project

A New York nonprofit restoring the harbor with oyster reefs, aiming for one billion oysters by 2030 (nonprofit figures).

Active🌊 Ocean

Salt Marshes

Salt marshes are nurseries for more than 75% of fisheries species and buffer shorelines from erosion and floods (NOAA).

Active🌊 Ocean

Ocean Voyages Institute

A sailing nonprofit that has pulled more than 850,000 pounds of plastic from the Pacific Gyre in 10 voyages (nonprofit figure).

Active🌊 Ocean

Healthy Seas

Volunteer divers recover ghost nets: Healthy Seas reports 1,309.1 tons of nets and marine litter removed since 2013 (Healthy Seas figure).

Active🌊 Ocean

Mote Marine Laboratory

Mote scientists have restored more than 216,000 corals to Florida's Coral Reef, and their restored corals now spawn (Mote figure).

Active🌊 Ocean

Seabin

Seabin's floating bins have captured 33.0 million items in Sydney Harbour since 2020, including 18.9 million microplastics (company figures).
