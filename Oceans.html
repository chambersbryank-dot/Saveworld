Oceans

Impact Statement

Three groups on this hub pull plastic out of the ocean and the rivers that feed it. The Ocean Cleanup reports about 65 million kg removed from rivers and the ocean.

Organization source: The Ocean Cleanup

4ocean passed 50 million pounds (about 22.7 million kg) removed in February 2026.

Company source: 4ocean, 50 million pounds removed

Plastic Bank reports about 196 million kg of plastic recovered through its collector network.

Company source: Plastic Bank newsletter, June 2026

Together that is more than 280 million kg of plastic kept out of the sea.

The Five W's of Ocean Solutions

Who

The Ocean Cleanup (nonprofit), 4ocean (company) and Plastic Bank (social enterprise).

What

Removing plastic from the open ocean, coastlines and rivers, and paying people to collect it before it reaches the sea.

When

Ongoing. 4ocean passed 50 million pounds removed in February 2026.

Company source: 4ocean, 50 million pounds removed

Where

Ocean garbage patches, coastlines and the rivers that carry plastic to the sea.

Why

Plastic in the ocean breaks into smaller pieces that are far harder to recover. Removing it early, or stopping it upstream, keeps it out of food chains.

Missions and Solutions

4ocean

4ocean removes plastic from oceans, rivers, and coastlines with full-time cleanup crews, funded by products made from recovered plastic.

The Ocean Cleanup

The Ocean Cleanup removes plastic from oceans and intercepts it in rivers.

Plastic Bank

Plastic Bank turns ocean-bound plastic into income for collectors in vulnerable communities.

Phytoplankton, the Ocean's Oxygen Engine

Natural solution

Impact Statement

About half of Earth's oxygen comes from the ocean, and most of it is made by phytoplankton, drifting plants and algae too small to see one by one. A single kind, Prochlorococcus, produces up to 20% of the oxygen in our whole biosphere. Keeping the ocean healthy keeps this oxygen engine running.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

For Further Information: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

About half of Earth's oxygen comes from the ocean, most of it made by tiny phytoplankton.

The Five W's of Phytoplankton

Who

Phytoplankton, drifting plants, algae and bacteria that photosynthesize near the ocean surface.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

What

A natural system that turns sunlight and carbon dioxide into food and oxygen.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

When

Every day; marine life uses about as much oxygen as phytoplankton produce.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

Where

The sunlit upper layer of every ocean; satellites like NASA's PACE track it from space.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

Source: NASA, PACE mission

Why

Healthy oceans mean breathable air and a working food web.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

Coral Restoration Foundation

Nonprofit solution · active

Impact Statement

Coral Restoration Foundation grows corals in offshore nurseries and replants them on the reef: it had returned 253,833 corals to the wild by the end of 2024, and rehomed 22,404 more across 11 reef sites in 2025 (organization figures).

Source: Coral Restoration Foundation, 2024 annual report (organization figure)

Source: Coral Restoration Foundation, 2025 annual report (organization figure)

For Further Information: Coral Restoration Foundation, restoration

Coral Restoration Foundation had returned 253,833 nursery-grown corals to Florida's reefs by the end of 2024 (organization figure).

The Five W's of Coral Restoration Foundation

Who

Coral Restoration Foundation, a nonprofit working in the Florida Keys.

Source: Coral Restoration Foundation, restoration

What

Offshore coral nurseries and large-scale outplanting of nursery-grown corals.

Source: Coral Restoration Foundation, restoration

When

Ongoing; 22,404 corals rehomed in 2025 (organization figure).

Source: Coral Restoration Foundation, 2025 annual report (organization figure)

Where

Florida's Coral Reef in the Florida Keys.

Source: Coral Restoration Foundation, restoration

Why

Replanting nursery-grown corals helps damaged reefs recover faster than they could alone.

Source: Coral Restoration Foundation, restoration
