ClearSpace

Europe's first active debris removal — capturing dead satellites and rocket parts from orbit.

Impact Statement

ClearSpace is proving that debris removal can move from demonstration to routine service, backed by ESA and national space agencies. Every captured object is one fewer collision risk for satellites, crewed missions, and the telescopes watching the sky.

The Five W's of ClearSpace

Who

ClearSpace, founded in 2018 as a spin-off from EPFL in Switzerland, headquartered in Luxembourg with offices across Switzerland, the U.K., and Germany. It is a privately held in-orbit servicing company.

What

Robotic servicers that find, capture, and deorbit space debris, plus inspection and life-extension missions. ClearSpace-1 uses four robotic arms to grapple a non-responsive object and drag it into a controlled reentry.

When

ClearSpace-1 was commissioned by ESA as the world's first active debris removal mission. Its original target, a Vega rocket adapter left in The curved path an object takes as it travels around a planet, moon or star, like a satellite circling Earth. Glossary in 2013, was replaced by ESA's retired PROBA-1 satellite. ClearSpace-1 is targeted for 2028 (ESA lists 2029). The PRELUDE mission, planned for launch in June 2027, will validate close-proximity rendezvous technology first.

Where

Low Earth orbit for debris removal, with geostationary-orbit life-extension missions planned. ClearSpace is also developing CLEAR with the U.K. Space Agency, a mission to remove two UK-registered objects; it is not yet confirmed (see On the horizon).

Why

Large debris objects are the seeds of future collisions. Removing them before they fragment prevents the Kessler syndrome — a runaway cascade that could make key orbits unusable for generations.
