The Great Bubble Barrier

A curtain of bubbles catching plastic across the full width and depth of rivers.

The Five W's of The Great Bubble Barrier

Who

The Great Bubble Barrier, a Dutch social enterprise based in Amsterdam. It operates as a limited company rather than a charity, blending mission with investment so it can scale technology and attract funding.

What

A bubble screen installed across a river or canal that creates an upward current catching plastic at the surface and below, guiding it to a collection point — without blocking ship traffic or fish migration.

When

First piloted in Katwijk in 2018. Bubble Barrier Amsterdam launched in 2019 and has captured over 1 million pieces of plastic. Additional barriers now operate in Harlingen and other Dutch locations.

Where

Rivers, canals, and waterways in the Netherlands flowing toward the North Sea and Wadden Sea, with feasibility studies and community toolkits helping other cities replicate the model.

Why

Rivers are the highways carrying plastic to the ocean, and most solutions either block navigation or miss submerged debris. Bubbles cover the full width and depth, work continuously, and need no moving parts in the water.

Impact Statement

It is a permanent, low-maintenance installation rather than a recurring cleanup cost. The company also runs awareness campaigns and advocates for policy change so cities treat river interception as standard infrastructure.
