Physics Explained

Share to Google Classroom(opens in a new tab)

Pushes and crashes, swings, magnets and the northern lights, hot and cold, and why GPS needs Einstein, in plain language.

Standards:

  • NGSS HS-PS2-1
  • NGSS MS-PS2-2
  • NGSS HS-PS3-1

Our alignment; not reviewed by the standards bodies.

Pushes and crashes

  • Kids

Things keep doing what they are doing until something pushes them. A heavy thing is harder to speed up than a light one. When two carts crash, whatever "oomph" one loses, the other gains.

Check yourself: If a big cart hits a small cart that is standing still, which one moves faster afterwards?

The small one: in a bouncy crash, a 2 kg cart at 3 m/s sends a 1 kg cart off at 4 m/s.

Swings and pendulums

  • Kids

A pendulum 1 m long takes about 2 seconds to swing there and back, whatever it weighs. Longer pendulums swing more slowly, which is why old clocks used them to keep time.

Check yourself: Does a heavier swing go back and forth faster?

No. Only the length (and gravity) sets the time, as long as the swings are small.

Air resistance

  • Teens

A ball thrown at 40 m/s (90 mph) would fly about 153 m with no air. With air pushing back, it only reaches about 94 m. Drag grows with the square of speed, so fast objects feel much more of it.

Check yourself: With air, is 45° still the best angle to throw for distance?

No: for this ball the best angle is about 41°, a bit lower.

Magnets and the northern lights

  • Teens

The Sun blows out a wind of charged particles at about 400 km/s. Earth’s magnetic field makes them spiral along its field lines toward the poles, where they hit the upper air and make it glow: the aurora.

Check yourself: Why are auroras seen mostly near the poles?

That is where Earth’s magnetic field lines dip down into the atmosphere, guiding the particles in.

Why GPS needs Einstein

  • Community college

GPS satellites carry atomic clocks. Because they are high up where gravity is weaker, those clocks gain about 46 microseconds a day; because they move fast, they lose about 7. The net gain of about 38 microseconds a day would add roughly 12 km of position error every day if it were not corrected.

Check yourself: Which effect wins for GPS: speed or gravity?

Gravity: the clocks run fast overall, so they are set slightly slow before launch.
