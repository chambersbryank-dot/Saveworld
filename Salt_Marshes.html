Salt Marshes

Salt marshes are nurseries for more than 75% of fisheries species and buffer shorelines from erosion and floods (NOAA).

Image: NASA/KSC (KSC-99WL10).

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • What is it? Coastal wetlands flooded and drained by the tides, with deep, spongy peat soils.
  • Why does it matter? They are essential for healthy fisheries, coastlines and communities.
  • How does it help people? A coastal wetland of grasses that floods with salty tide water. It soaks up storm surges and stores carbon in its mud. Glossary reduce flooding by slowing and absorbing rainwater, protect shorelines from erosion, and support the seafood many communities depend on.

The Five W's of Salt Marshes

Who

Nature, described by NOAA’s National Ocean Service.

Source: NOAA Ocean Service, What is a salt marsh?

What

Coastal wetlands flooded and drained by the tides, with deep, spongy peat soils.

Source: NOAA Ocean Service, What is a salt marsh?

When

Twice a day with the tides, all year.

Source: NOAA Ocean Service, What is a salt marsh?

Where

Worldwide, especially at middle to high latitudes; in the U.S. on every coast, with about half along the Gulf Coast.

Source: NOAA Ocean Service, What is a salt marsh?

Why

They are essential for healthy fisheries, coastlines and communities.

Source: NOAA Ocean Service, What is a salt marsh?

Impact Statement

Natural solution

Salt marshes provide food, refuge or nursery habitat for more than 75% of fisheries species, including shrimp, blue crab and many finfish. They protect shorelines from erosion by buffering waves and trapping Sand, mud and gravel carried by water that settles on river beds, lake bottoms and beaches. Glossary.

Source: NOAA Ocean Service, What is a salt marsh?

For Further Information: NOAA Ocean Service, What is a salt marsh?

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Salt marshes reduce flooding by slowing and absorbing rainwater, protect shorelines from erosion, and support the seafood many communities depend on.

Source: NOAA Ocean Service, What is a salt marsh?

For Nature

They filter runoff and metabolize excess nutrients to protect water quality, and shelter young fish and shellfish. See also A tree that grows in salty water along tropical coasts. Its tangled roots protect shores from waves and shelter young fish. Glossary Forests and Flowering plants that grow underwater in shallow seas, forming meadows that shelter animals and store carbon.
