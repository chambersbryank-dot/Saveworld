Hayabusa2 Torifune Flyby (JAXA)

On 5 July 2026, JAXA's Hayabusa2 passed about 400 m above asteroid Torifune, the closest flyby of any Solar System body so far (JAXA).

The Five W's of Hayabusa2 Torifune Flyby (JAXA)

Who

The Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) and its industry and university partners.

Source: JAXA, Hayabusa2 sets a new record for closest flyby

What

A high-speed flyby of the small asteroid Torifune during Hayabusa2's extended mission.

Source: JAXA, Hayabusa2 captures images of asteroid Torifune

When

5 July 2026; Hayabusa2 launched in 2014 and delivered asteroid Ryugu samples to Earth in 2020.

Source: JAXA, Hayabusa2 captures images of asteroid Torifune

Where

Deep space; Hayabusa2 passed about 744 m from Torifune's center at about 5 km per second.

Source: JAXA, Hayabusa2 sets a new record for closest flyby

Why

Steering a spacecraft precisely to a tiny, fast asteroid is the skill needed to deflect a dangerous one.

Source: JAXA, Hayabusa2 sets a new record for closest flyby

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

Hayabusa2 came within about 400 m of Torifune's surface and missed its aim point by only 120 m. JAXA says this shows control technology able to make a spacecraft hit a small asteroid, a major step for planetary defense.

Source: JAXA, Hayabusa2 sets a new record for closest flyby

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Precise navigation like this is what a future mission would need to push a threatening asteroid away from Earth.

Source: JAXA, Hayabusa2 sets a new record for closest flyby

For Nature

Studying small asteroids up close tells scientists what the early Solar System was made of.
