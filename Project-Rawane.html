Project Rawane

A two-way relationship between Earth and Space. Project R is named for Rawane — because the best projects start with someone you love.

Impact Statement

It follows Errol Scales' work on orbital debris removal. That work matters, considering there are over 1.5 million pieces of debris ranging from one centimeter to the size of a Greyhound bus orbiting Earth at speeds up to 18,000 miles per hour (ESA Space Debris Office, MASTER-8, Feb 2026). Space disposal solutions are moving forward.

Project Rawane rolls the whole portfolio into one page: space disposal made by mankind and by nature, planetary defense, space weather, and the voyages outward that bring knowledge home. ADRAS-J2, Phase II of JAXA's Commercial Removal of Debris Demonstration (JAXA), aims to capture and deorbit an 11-meter, 3-ton H-IIA upper stage, the world's first removal of legacy debris (Astroscale, company figures). ESA's Hera reaches the asteroid that DART moved in November 2026 (ESA), and ESA–JAXA Ramses (ESA) will meet Apophis before its April 13, 2029 pass about 20,000 miles from Earth (NASA). NASA's IMAP can give about a half hour's warning of incoming solar radiation (NASA), while PUNCH maps how the Sun's corona becomes the solar wind (NASA). Artemis II carried four people 252,756 miles from Earth, the farthest humans have gone (NASA), and Europa Clipper, with 49 Europa flybys ahead (NASA), and Juice, with 35 moon flybys (ESA), are bound for Jupiter's ocean moons.

It updates every day. The live launch widget pulls the next launches from The Space Devs' Launch Library 2, mission-status widgets count down to each next milestone with the latest news, new sourced candidates are researched every morning, and the Artemis schedule refreshes weekly from NASA's Artemis pages.

And it is built to be shared. Every figure links to NASA, ESA, JAXA or another original source, the page reads in English, French, Spanish and Arabic, and the strongest facts are collected on Amazing Facts. The Five W's of Project Rawane

Who

Saving Planets, rolling up missions by NASA, ESA, JAXA, ISRO, NOAA and CSA, with company partners such as Astroscale, Northrop Grumman and Starfish Space (company figures labeled).

Source: JAXA, CRD2 Phase II

What

One living roll-up of Project Rawane: space disposal made by mankind and by nature, planetary defense, space weather and voyages outward, each with a sourced Impact Statement, The Five W's and, where there is a schedule, a live status widget.

When

Every day. Launches and mission news refresh live, new candidates arrive from a daily research pipeline, and the Artemis schedule refreshes weekly. Next up: Hera's arrival in November 2026 and Apophis' close pass on April 13, 2029.

Source: ESA, Hera

Where

From low Earth orbit to the Moon, Mars, Jupiter's moons and the Sun–Earth L1 point, and shared worldwide in four languages.

Why

Space and Earth are one system. Debris threatens the satellites we rely on, solar storms threaten power grids, and every voyage outward shows how precious home is. Current, sourced information helps more people understand the fixes, and support them.

Space Disposal

Man-Made Solutions

Robotic capture and deorbit of legacy debris — servicer spacecraft that rendezvous with dead rocket bodies and satellites, grab them with robotic arms, and lower their orbits so they burn up in the atmosphere.

Natural Solutions

Earth's upper atmosphere pulls low-orbit junk down to burn up, and Jupiter's gravity swallows or flings away comets and asteroids.

Space Disposal (Made by Mankind)

Astroscale

Astroscale inspects, services and removes debris in orbit: ELSA-d, ADRAS-J's 2024 close-up of an H-IIA upper stage, and ADRAS-J2, targeted for fiscal 2027.

ClearSpace

ClearSpace leads Europe's first active debris removal mission and provides in-orbit servicing.

LeoLabs

LeoLabs runs a global network of ground radars tracking satellites and debris in low Earth orbit, turning the data into collision warnings (company figures).

MMX

JAXA's MMX launches October 2026 to land on Mars's moon Phobos and return at least 10 grams of it to Earth in 2031, a first for the Mars system.

PRELUDE (ClearSpace) horizon

PRELUDE is a ClearSpace and ESA mission planned for June 2027 to validate close-proximity rendezvous technology. A potential solution on the horizon.

U.S. Deorbit Vehicle (NASA / SpaceX)

NASA selected SpaceX to build the U.S. Deorbit Vehicle that will guide the International Space Station safely out of orbit after 2030.

Aeolus Assisted Reentry (ESA)

ESA guided its retired Aeolus wind satellite down in the first assisted reentry of its kind, cutting the already low debris risk by a factor of 150.

LignoSat Wooden Satellite (Kyoto University) horizon

LignoSat, the world's first wooden satellite, proved wood can survive in orbit; a follow-on is a potential solution on the horizon.

Northrop Grumman SpaceLogistics

Life extension in GEO: two MEVs have docked with three satellites, and the Mission Robotic Vehicle and three pods launched July 21, 2026 (company figures).

Starfish Space Otter

The first Otter launched October 1, 2026 for NASA's SSPICY mission, inspecting up to four dead satellites and rocket bodies from 2027.

Space Disposal (Made by Nature)

Atmospheric Drag

Atmospheric drag is a natural space debris removal system: it pulls low-orbit junk down to burn up, faster when the Sun is active, but barely works high up.

Titan

Titan is a prebiotic time capsule, and NASA's Dragonfly rotorcraft explores it from 2034, building radar, power and autonomy tools that matter for Earth.

Jupiter

Jupiter's gravity captures or flings away comets and asteroids, a natural debris-removal system on a scale no human mission can match.

Europa Clipper

NASA's Europa Clipper is on its way to Jupiter's ocean moon, with an Earth gravity assist on December 3, 2026 and 49 close Europa flybys from 2030.

Juice

ESA's Juice will study Ganymede, Callisto and Europa, with 35 moon flybys after it reaches Jupiter in July 2031.

Planetary Defense

Hera

ESA's Hera arrives at the asteroid Dimorphos in November 2026 to measure what NASA's DART impact did, turning deflection into a repeatable defense.

Jupiter

Comet impacts are thought to happen over 2,000 times more often on Jupiter than on Earth, and 10-meter objects hit it 12 to 45 times a year, a live lab for planetary defense.

NEO Surveyor

NASA NEO Surveyor is the first space telescope built to find hazardous asteroids, including dark ones hidden in the Sun's glare. Launch NET September 2027.

Webb and 2024 YR4

How the James Webb Space Telescope tracked asteroid 2024 YR4 in February 2026 and ruled out a 2032 Moon impact, two years before ground telescopes could.

OSIRIS-APEX and Apophis 2029 (NASA)

On April 13, 2029, asteroid Apophis passes about 20,000 miles from Earth. NASA's OSIRIS-APEX meets it in June 2029 to see how that close pass changes it.

Ramses (ESA / JAXA)

ESA and JAXA's Ramses must launch by spring 2028 to meet Apophis before its 2029 Earth pass. Its €81.2 million build contract was signed in February 2026.

Space Weather & Shields

Aditya-L1 (ISRO)

Aditya-L1 is ISRO's first space-based solar observatory, studying the Sun from a halo orbit around L1, about 1.5 million km from Earth.

SOLAR-1 (NOAA)

NOAA's SOLAR-1 (formerly SWFO-L1) watches the Sun from L1, about one million miles from Earth, streaming space weather data to forecasters in real time.

Three Invisible Shields

Three natural shields protect Earth: the heliosphere, the magnetosphere, and the atmosphere and ozone layer.

Vigil (ESA)

ESA's Vigil will watch the Sun from L5, giving up to four to five days' notice of certain space weather effects. Launch is planned for 2031.

IMAP (NASA)

NASA's IMAP watches the Sun from about a million miles sunward and can give about a half hour's warning of incoming radiation.

GOES-19 and CCOR-1 (NOAA)

NOAA's GOES-19 carries CCOR-1, the world's first operational space-based coronagraph, imaging the Sun's corona every 15 minutes.

PUNCH (NASA)

Four suitcase-sized NASA satellites image the Sun's corona and solar wind together, in 3D, since March 2025.

TRACERS (NASA)

NASA's twin TRACERS satellites fly through Earth's polar cusps to study magnetic reconnection, a key driver of space weather. Linked to the magnetic shield on Three Shields.

Carruthers Geocorona Observatory (NASA)

The first mission dedicated to Earth's exosphere, Carruthers watches our planet's ultraviolet halo from about a million miles sunward.

Solar Orbiter (ESA / NASA)

ESA and NASA's Solar Orbiter took the first close-up images of the Sun's poles, coming within 42 million km of the Sun.

Voyages Outward

Artemis II (NASA / CSA)

Artemis II carried four astronauts around the Moon, reaching 252,756 miles from Earth, farther than any humans before.

ESCAPADE (NASA)

NASA's twin ESCAPADE spacecraft will study how the solar wind strips Mars' atmosphere, after an Earth slingshot in fall 2026.

Artemis Program

Artemis I

Flown November–December 2022

NASA's first flight test of SLS, Orion and ground systems: an uncrewed 25-day trip beyond the Moon, launched Nov. 16 and splashed down Dec. 11, 2022.

Source: NASA, Artemis I

Artemis II

Flown April 2026

Four astronauts flew around the Moon in April 2026 and reached 252,756 miles from Earth, farther than any humans before.

Source: NASA, Artemis II crew back on Earth

Artemis III

NASA target: 2027

A crewed test in low Earth orbit: Orion practices docking with commercial lunar landers before any landing.

Source: NASA, Artemis III mission

Artemis IV

NASA target: early 2028

Humanity's return to the lunar surface: two astronauts spend about a week near the Moon's South Pole.

Source: NASA, Artemis IV

Artemis V

NASA target: late 2028

A second astronaut landing in NASA's February 2026 plan.

Source: NASA, Return to the lunar surface plan (Mar 2026)
