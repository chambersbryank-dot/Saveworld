Global Fishing Watch

Global Fishing Watch uses satellites and AI to map fishing; its 2024 Nature study found about 75% of industrial fishing vessels are not publicly tracked (Global Fishing Watch).

Image: NASA (iss073e0257194).

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • Big fishing ships sail all over the ocean, and many of them are hard to see.
  • Global Fishing Watch uses pictures from space and smart computers to find them.
  • They put the map online for free, so everyone can see where fishing happens and protect the ocean.

The Five W's of Global Fishing Watch

Who

Global Fishing Watch, an independent nonprofit founded by Oceana, SkyTruth and Google.

Source: Global Fishing Watch, About Us

What

Free public maps and data that show where boats fish, built from vessel GPS signals, satellite images and machine learning.

Source: Global Fishing Watch, Support Us

When

Founded in 2015; its first public fishing map came out in 2016, and it became independent in 2017.

Source: Global Fishing Watch, About Us

Where

The global ocean.

Source: Global Fishing Watch, About Us

Why

The ocean is the least observed part of our planet, so overfishing and illegal fishing can stay hidden.

Source: Global Fishing Watch, About Us

Impact Statement

Nonprofit solution · active

A Global Fishing Watch-led study in Nature analyzed 2 million gigabytes of satellite images and found about 75% of the world's industrial fishing vessels are not publicly tracked, including many inside marine protected areas.

Source: Global Fishing Watch, Nature study on industrial activity at sea

For Further Information: Global Fishing Watch, Nature study on industrial activity at sea

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Open data helps protect fisheries and the livelihoods of small-scale fishers, and shows where industrial boats may be entering local fishing grounds.

Source: Global Fishing Watch, Nature study on industrial activity at sea

For Nature

Revealing "dark" vessels helps governments enforce marine protected areas and protect The variety of all living things in a place: plants, animals, fungi and tiny microbes. Glossary.

Source: Global Fishing Watch, About Us
