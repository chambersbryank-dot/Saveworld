Saharan Dust Feeds the Amazon

Every year about 27.7 million tons of Saharan dust falls on the Amazon, delivering some 22,000 tons of phosphorus that the rainforest needs (NASA).

Image: NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center.

  1. Home
  2. Forests

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 3 sources cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • What is it? Wind carries phosphorus-rich dust from the Sahara across the Atlantic to fertilize the Amazon.
  • Why does it matter? Rain washes phosphorus out of Amazon soils; the dust replaces it.
  • How does it help people? A healthy Amazon matters to people far beyond Brazil: its forest helps make its own rain (see The Amazon Makes Its Own Rainy Season), and Saharan dust keeps that forest fed.

The Five W's of Saharan Dust Feeds the Amazon

Who

Nature, measured by NASA’s CALIPSO satellite and scientists at NASA Goddard.

Source: NASA, Saharan dust feeds the Amazon

What

Wind carries phosphorus-rich dust from the Sahara across the Atlantic to fertilize the Amazon.

Source: NASA, Saharan dust feeds the Amazon

When

Every year; the satellite estimate covers multiple years of data.

Source: NASA, Saharan dust feeds the Amazon

Source: Yu et al. 2015, Geophysical Research Letters

Where

Much of the dust comes from the Bodélé Depression in Chad, an ancient lake bed, and falls over the Amazon basin.

Source: NASA, Saharan dust feeds the Amazon

Why

Rain washes phosphorus out of Amazon soils; the dust replaces it.

Source: NASA, Saharan dust feeds the Amazon

Impact Statement

Natural solution

On average wind lifts 182 million tons of dust a year past the western edge of the Sahara, and 27.7 million tons fall over the Amazon basin. The estimated 22,000 tons of phosphorus it brings each year is about the same amount the Amazon loses to rain and flooding.

Source: NASA, Saharan dust feeds the Amazon

Source: Yu et al. 2015, Geophysical Research Letters

For Further Information: NASA, Saharan dust feeds the Amazon

About 27.7 million tons of Saharan dust fall on the Amazon each year, enough to fill 104,908 semi trucks.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

A healthy Amazon matters to people far beyond Brazil: its forest helps make its own rain (see The Amazon Makes Its Own Rainy Season), and Saharan dust keeps that forest fed.

Source: NASA, Saharan dust feeds the Amazon

Source: NASA JPL, the Amazon makes its own rainy season

For Nature

Phosphorus is an essential nutrient for plant growth. Dust from one of the planet’s most desolate places replaces what the rain washes away in one of its most fertile.

Source: NASA, Saharan dust feeds the Amazon

Want to go deeper? NASA's story has the satellite data and visualizations.
