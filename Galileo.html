Galileo Satellite Navigation (EU / ESA)

Europe's Galileo is the world's most precise satellite navigation system, serving over five billion smartphone users and helping rescues (ESA).

NASA (ISS074-E-0149572, Jan. 18, 2026)NASA/JPL/University of Arizona/University of Idaho (PIA20016)NASA/JPL-Caltech (PIA25790)NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute (PIA11688)

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • Galileo is a group of satellites that tells your phone exactly where it is.
  • Each satellite has very accurate atomic clocks.
  • Galileo satellites can also hear emergency beacons and pass the alert to rescuers.

The Five W's of Galileo

Who

The European Union owns and funds Galileo through the European Commission. ESA designs and builds it, and EUSPA runs the service.

Source: ESA, Two new Galileo satellites enter service

What

A constellation designed for 30 satellites, 3 of them spares. Each carries atomic clocks and a search-and-rescue antenna.

Source: ESA, Galileo satellites

When

Open Service began in 2016. Satellites SAT 33 and SAT 34 entered service in 2026, and four first-generation satellites remain to launch.

Source: ESA, Two new Galileo satellites enter service

Where

In medium Earth The curved path an object takes as it travels around a planet, moon or star, like a satellite circling Earth. Glossary, at 23,222 km.

Source: ESA, Two new Galileo satellites enter service

Why

Precise positioning and timing matter for rail, shipping, farming, finance and rescue operations (ESA).

Source: ESA, Two new Galileo satellites enter service

Connected Solutions

  • SARSAT The international distress-beacon system that Galileo's rescue antennas feed into.

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

Galileo serves over five billion smartphone users around the globe, and all smartphones sold in the EU single market are guaranteed Galileo-enabled (ESA).

Source: ESA, Two new Galileo satellites enter service

For Further Information: ESA, Two new Galileo satellites enter service

Each Galileo satellite's hydrogen maser clock keeps time to within 0.45 billionths of a second over 12 hours (ESA).

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Galileo's search-and-rescue antenna picks up distress signals from beacons and sends them on to local rescue services (ESA).

Source: ESA, Galileo satellites

For Nature

Precise positioning supports farming, one of the fields where ESA says Galileo is making a difference (ESA).

Source: ESA, Two new Galileo satellites enter service
