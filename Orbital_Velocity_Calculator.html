Orbital Velocity & Escape Velocity Calculator

Share to Google Classroom(opens in a new tab)

Orbital velocity is the speed needed to stay in a circular orbit at a given height. Escape velocity is the speed needed to leave Earth for good without more thrust. Escape velocity is always √2 times the circular speed.

Standards:

  • NGSS HS-ESS1-4
  • NGSS HS-PS2-4
  • IB D.1

Our alignment; not reviewed by the standards bodies.

Formulas verified in build: 2026-10-16

  • Circular orbital velocity7.669 km/s
  • Escape velocity10.845 km/s

Worked example

Given
  • Altitude above Earth (km): 400 km
Answer
  • Circular orbital velocity: 7.669 km/s
  • Escape velocity: 10.845 km/s
Check
At the surface this gives 11.18 km/s for escape and 7.905 km/s for circular speed. NASA’s Earth fact sheet lists 11.186 km/s because it uses the 6,371 km mean radius instead of the 6,378.137 km equatorial radius used here. The build checks both.

Assumptions

Spherical Earth, no air drag, no Earth rotation bonus.
