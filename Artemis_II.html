Artemis II

Four astronauts, one loop around the Moon, and a new record for how far humans have traveled.

Image: NASA, Artemis II launch.

Artemis II: Mission Status

Flown. Four astronauts (NASA's Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover and Christina Koch, and CSA's Jeremy Hansen) flew around the Moon, reached 252,756 miles from Earth on April 6, 2026, and splashed down off San Diego after a nearly 10-day, 695,081-mile journey.

Artemis III: Mission Status

NASA target: 2027 (as of October 2026; no launch date set, so no countdown yet). A crewed test flight in low Earth orbit: Orion will practice rendezvous and docking with one or both commercial lunar landers from SpaceX and Blue Origin, and test new spacesuits, before any landing.

Artemis IV: Mission Status

NASA target: early 2028 (as of October 2026). Humanity's return to the lunar surface: two of four crew will descend near the Moon's South Pole and spend about a week there. Crew not yet announced.

Artemis II · Flown

Crew: Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch (NASA) and Jeremy Hansen (CSA). Hardware: SLS rocket and Orion spacecraft. Result: the farthest humans have traveled, 252,756 miles, breaking Apollo 13's 1970 record.

Artemis III · NASA Target 2027

Goal: test Orion's rendezvous and docking with commercial landers, life support, communications, propulsion and new xEVA spacesuits in low Earth orbit. Crew: Randy Bresnik (commander), Luca Parmitano (ESA, pilot), Andre Douglas and Frank Rubio (mission specialists). Hardware: SLS, Orion, and SpaceX's Starship and/or Blue Origin's Blue Moon landers.

Source: NASA, Artemis architecture update

Source: NASA, Artemis III crew

Artemis IV · NASA Target Early 2028

Goal: land two astronauts near the lunar South Pole for about a week of science. Hardware: SLS (standardized configuration from Artemis IV), Orion docking with a commercial human landing system in lunar orbit. Crew: not yet announced.

Source: NASA, Artemis IV

Source: NASA, Artemis program

The Five W's of Artemis II

Who

NASA and the Canadian Space Agency, with a crew of four.

Source: NASA, Artemis II crew back on Earth

What

A crewed test flight of the Orion spacecraft and Space Launch System around the Moon.

Source: NASA, Artemis II mission

When

April 2026; the crew splashed down off San Diego after a nearly 10-day journey.

Source: NASA, Artemis II crew back on Earth

Where

A loop around the Moon and back, about 695,081 miles in all.

Source: NASA, Artemis II distance record

Why

To prove Orion with people aboard before Artemis III, NASA's next lunar mission.

Source: NASA, Artemis II mission

Impact Statement

Agency mission · complete; Artemis III next

Artemis II was the first crewed flight around the Moon in more than half a century. NASA astronauts Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover and Christina Koch and CSA astronaut Jeremy Hansen reached 252,756 miles from Earth on April 6, 2026, breaking Apollo 13's 1970 record, and flew 695,081 miles in total. It is the relationship between Earth and space at its most human: the farther we go, the more clearly we see home.

Why Voyages Outward Matter on Earth

Every Artemis flight is a round trip. Orion is the only spacecraft able to bring crews home at lunar reentry speeds, and the whole program is built to return people safely to the planet they left. The farther the Artemis II crew traveled, the smaller Earth looked, and the clearer it became that there is one home worth protecting.

Source: NASA, Artemis IV

That is the thread of Project Rawane: the same skills that let us fly to the Moon (precise rendezvous, life support, guiding spacecraft safely home) are the skills that clean up orbit, defend Earth from asteroids and watch over our oceans and forests.
