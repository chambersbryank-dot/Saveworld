Astroscale

On-orbit servicing: inspect, extend, refuel, and remove.

The Five W's of Astroscale

Who

Astroscale, founded 4 May 2013 in Singapore by Mitsunobu "Nobu" Okada. Headquarters are in Tokyo. It is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Growth market, and it has offices in the United Kingdom, the United States, France, and Israel.

What

On-orbit servicing: magnetic capture for end-of-life satellites, close inspection of debris, and planned robotic removal, life extension, and refueling.

When

ELSA-d launched 22 March 2021 and tested magnetic capture. ADRAS-J launched in February 2024 and, in November 2024, approached to about 15 meters from a discarded Japanese H-2A upper stage. JAXA has contracted ADRAS-J2 to remove that same rocket body. Published launch targets differ; one company profile puts the removal launch in fiscal 2028.

Where

Low Earth orbit for the debris missions flown so far. Geostationary life-extension and a U.S. Space Force refueling mission are in development. Customers include JAXA and other government agencies.

Why

Large dead objects are the seeds of collisions. Inspecting and removing them keeps orbits usable for satellites and future missions.

Impact Statement

ADRAS-J was the first spacecraft to approach and photograph a large piece of debris up close. The next contracted step is to remove that rocket body, not only inspect it. Launch-year figures for ADRAS-J2 are targets, not a flown date.
