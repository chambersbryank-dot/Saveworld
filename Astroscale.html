Astroscale

The pit crew for orbit — inspecting, servicing, and removing space debris.

The Five W's of Astroscale

Who

Astroscale, founded in 2013 by Nobu Okada and headquartered in Tokyo, with subsidiaries in the U.S., U.K., France, and Israel. It is publicly listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

What

On-orbit servicing across the full lifecycle: life extension, refueling, inspection, end-of-life disposal, and active debris removal. Their spacecraft rendezvous with and capture defunct satellites and rocket bodies.

When

First launch in March 2021. ELSA-d demonstrated magnetic capture in 2021, ADRAS-J performed the first close inspection of a tumbling rocket body with JAXA in 2024, and ADRAS-J2 is contracted for a debris-removal launch.

Where

Low Earth orbit today, with geostationary-orbit life-extension services planned. Missions are funded by JAXA, ESA, the U.K. Space Agency, the U.S. Space Force, and commercial operators.

Why

Decades of launches have left millions of debris objects in orbit, and collisions create more debris — the Kessler syndrome. Removing large objects before they fragment is the only way to keep orbits usable for satellites, science, and future missions.

Impact Statement

Astroscale is building the service layer that makes a circular space economy possible — so operators can inspect, repair, and dispose of spacecraft instead of abandoning them. Every successful removal reduces collision risk for everything else in orbit.
