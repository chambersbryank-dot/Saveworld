TEMPO Air Quality Mission (NASA)

From geostationary orbit, NASA's TEMPO measures air pollution over North America every daylight hour, a first from space (NASA).

Image: NASA.

  1. Home
  2. Earth Observation
  3. TEMPO Air Quality Mission (NASA)

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 2 sources cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • TEMPO is a space tool that checks how dirty the air is.
  • It stays above the same spot on Earth and looks at North America every hour while the sun is up.
  • This helps warn people on bad air days so they can stay safe.

The Five W's of TEMPO Air Quality Mission (NASA)

Who

NASA, with data processed by the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory.

Source: NASA, TEMPO instrument launches

What

TEMPO, a spectrometer that measures air pollutants such as A gas high in the sky that blocks harmful ultraviolet rays from the Sun. Near the ground, ozone is a pollutant. Glossary and nitrogen dioxide.

Source: NASA Earthdata, TEMPO

When

Launched April 7, 2023, on the Intelsat 40e satellite aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9.

Source: NASA, TEMPO instrument launches

Where

About 22,000 miles above the equator, watching North America from the Atlantic to the Pacific.

Source: NASA Earthdata, TEMPO

Why

Air pollution changes hour by hour, but older satellites passed over only about once a day.

Source: NASA, TEMPO instrument launches

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

TEMPO is the first space instrument to measure North American air quality every daylight hour, at a resolution of a few square miles instead of about 100 square miles.

Source: NASA, TEMPO instrument launches

For Further Information: NASA, TEMPO instrument launches

NASA's TEMPO checks North America's air pollution every daylight hour from 22,000 miles up.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Hourly data can improve air quality alerts and shows rush-hour pollution and smoke from fires moving toward cities.

Source: NASA, TEMPO instrument launches

For Nature

It tracks pollution from forest fires and volcanoes and helps scientists study ozone and the effects of fertilizer.

Source: NASA, TEMPO instrument launches
