Catalina Sky Survey

A NASA-funded survey in Arizona that has discovered more near-Earth objects than any other survey in history: 17,918 near-Earth asteroids since 1995 (Catalina Sky Survey).

Image: NASA/JPL-Caltech/CSS-Univ. of Arizona (PIA22468).

The Five W's of Catalina Sky Survey

Who

The Catalina Sky Survey, a NASA-funded project of NASA's Planetary Defense Coordination Office, based at the University of Arizona's Lunar and Planetary Laboratory.

Source: Catalina Sky Survey

What

A mission fully dedicated to discovering and tracking near-Earth objects, using survey telescopes with large 10k x 10k cameras.

Source: Catalina Sky Survey, Discovery statistics

When

Active since the late 1990s, with big growth in discoveries from 2004-2005 and after camera upgrades in 2016.

Source: Catalina Sky Survey, Discovery statistics

Where

Tucson, Arizona.

Source: Catalina Sky Survey

Why

Finding and tracking near-Earth objects is the first step in planetary defense.

Source: Catalina Sky Survey

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

The Catalina Sky Survey has discovered 17,918 near-Earth A rocky object that orbits the Sun, much smaller than a planet. Most are in a belt between Mars and Jupiter. Glossary from 1995 to the present, more than any other survey project in history (Catalina Sky Survey).

Source: Catalina Sky Survey

For Further Information: Catalina Sky Survey

Camera upgrades in 2016 at Catalina's 1.5 m and 0.7 m telescopes led to another great leap in near-Earth object discoveries (Catalina Sky Survey).

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Early discovery of near-Earth objects gives time for warnings and, if needed, action to protect people (Catalina Sky Survey).

Source: Catalina Sky Survey

For Nature

Discovering and tracking near-Earth objects is the first step in protecting Earth, its people and its wildlife from an impact (Catalina Sky Survey).

Source: Catalina Sky Survey
