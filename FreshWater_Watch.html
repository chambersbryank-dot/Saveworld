FreshWater Watch (Earthwatch Europe)

FreshWater Watch trains citizen scientists to test rivers, lakes and ponds: 80,000+ datasets from 22,000+ volunteers (organization figures).

The Five W's of FreshWater Watch (Earthwatch Europe)

Who

FreshWater Watch, a global citizen science project created by the environmental charity Earthwatch Europe (registered charity no. 1094467).

Source: FreshWater Watch, Earthwatch Europe

What

Training groups and individuals to test water quality, for example nitrates and phosphates, and share the results.

Source: FreshWater Watch, Earthwatch Europe

When

So far 80,000+ datasets have been collected, uploaded and verified by 22,000+ citizen scientists.

Source: FreshWater Watch, Earthwatch Europe

Where

Rivers, lakes, streams, ponds, wetlands and reservoirs around the world.

Source: FreshWater Watch, Earthwatch Europe

Why

Nearly all freshwater places are affected by human-made pollution, and by 2050 half of the world's people may live in water-scarce areas.

Source: FreshWater Watch, Earthwatch Europe

Impact Statement

Nonprofit solution · active

FreshWater Watch reports more than 80,000 datasets from more than 22,000 citizen scientists, freely available to scientists, governments and policymakers (organization figures).

Source: FreshWater Watch, Earthwatch Europe

For Further Information: FreshWater Watch, Earthwatch Europe

More than 22,000 volunteers have tested fresh water for FreshWater Watch, creating a free global dataset.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Free water-quality data helps communities and decision-makers spot pollution, like a 2025 citizen study that found metals in rivers in Corby, England.

Source: FreshWater Watch, Earthwatch Europe

For Nature

Finding pollution early helps protect fish, insects and plants in rivers, lakes and wetlands.

Source: FreshWater Watch, Earthwatch Europe
