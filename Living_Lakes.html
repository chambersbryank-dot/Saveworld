Living Lakes (Global Nature Fund)

Since 1998, the Global Nature Fund's Living Lakes Network has protected and restored lakes and wetlands with 130 member organizations in more than 60 countries (Global Nature Fund).

Image: NASA (ISS071-E-14284).

  1. Home

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 2 sources cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • Living Lakes is a team of groups that look after lakes all over the world.
  • They clean up trash and help people near lakes earn money without harming nature.
  • In Mongolia they help protect a lake that is about 2 million years old.

The Five W's of Living Lakes

Who

The Global Nature Fund (GNF), a nature conservation foundation with offices in Radolfzell, Bonn and Berlin, Germany, and the 130 member organizations of the Living Lakes Network.

Source: Global Nature Fund, Living Lakes

What

Protecting and restoring lakes and wetlands: removing plastic waste from waterways, pushing for strong protections and creating sustainable incomes for local communities.

Source: Global Nature Fund, Living Lakes

When

Since 1998.

Source: Global Nature Fund, Living Lakes

Where

More than 60 countries. One project protects Lake Hovsgol in Mongolia's Hovsgol National Park with local partner ecoDevshilt.

Source: Global Nature Fund, Living Lakes

Why

Source: Global Nature Fund, Living Lakes

Impact Statement

Nonprofit solution · active

At Lake Hovsgol, a new waste management system keeps the national park clean and removes illegal dumps, while stronger local cooperatives help nomadic families earn a living.

Source: Global Nature Fund, Living Lakes

For Further Information: Global Nature Fund, Living Lakes

Living Lakes helps protect Lake Hovsgol, a lake about 2 million years old, and the nomadic families who live beside it.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

GNF combines nature protection with fighting poverty. Its drinking water projects have given 146,929 people access to clean water (organization figure).

Source: Global Nature Fund, About us

For Nature

Healthy lakes and wetlands are home to rich wildlife. At Hovsgol, cleaning up trash protects a sensitive ecosystem threatened by growing tourism.

Source: Global Nature Fund, Living Lakes
