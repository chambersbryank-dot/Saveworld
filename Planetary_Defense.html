Planetary Defense

Impact Statement

Find Them, Then Move Them

Planetary defense has two halves. First, find and track asteroids and comets early. Then, if one is ever on course for Earth, have a tested way to push it aside. Humanity has now done both: NASA's DART spacecraft hit the asteroid moonlet Dimorphos on September 26, 2022 and shortened its 11-hour-55-minute orbit by 33 minutes and 15 seconds.

Source: NASA, Asteroid's orbit, shape changed after DART impact

This hub collects the missions that make that defense real, plus the natural one that has been doing it for billions of years.

The Five W's of Planetary Defense

Who

NASA and ESA, plus the James Webb Space Telescope team, and Jupiter (natural).

What

Finding and tracking asteroids early, and testing ways to push one aside. DART changed Dimorphos's orbit by 33 minutes and 15 seconds.

Source: NASA, Asteroid's orbit, shape changed after DART impact

When

DART impact September 26, 2022; Hera is following up, and NEO Surveyor is in development.

Source: NASA, Asteroid's orbit, shape changed after DART impact

Where

Near-Earth space, the Didymos system and beyond.

Why

NEO Surveyor aims to find two-thirds of unknown near-Earth objects larger than 140 m within five years.

Source: NASA, NEO Surveyor launch services contract

Missions and Solutions

Hera

ESA's Hera arrives at the asteroid Dimorphos in November 2026 to measure what NASA's DART impact did, turning deflection into a repeatable defense.

Jupiter

Jupiter is a natural space debris removal system that swallows or flings away far more dangerous objects than any human mission could.

NEO Surveyor

NASA NEO Surveyor is the first space telescope built to find hazardous asteroids, including dark ones hidden in the Sun's glare. Launch NET September 2027.

Webb and 2024 YR4

How the James Webb Space Telescope tracked asteroid 2024 YR4 in February 2026 and ruled out a 2032 Moon impact, two years before ground telescopes could.

OSIRIS-APEX and Apophis 2029 (NASA)

On April 13, 2029, asteroid Apophis passes about 20,000 miles from Earth. NASA's OSIRIS-APEX meets it in June 2029 to see how that close pass changes it.

Ramses (ESA / JAXA)

ESA and JAXA's Ramses must launch by spring 2028 to meet Apophis before its 2029 Earth pass. Its €81.2 million build contract was signed in February 2026.

A Note on Roman

NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched on August 30, 2026. NASA describes it as a mission for dark energy, dark matter, exoplanets and galaxy surveys. It is not a near-Earth-object mission. The dedicated asteroid hunter is NEO Surveyor.

Source: NASA, Roman launch release

Source: NASA, NEO Surveyor

OSIRIS-APEX and Apophis 2029 (NASA)

Agency solution · active

Impact Statement

Near-Earth asteroid Apophis will safely pass about 20,000 miles (32,000 km) from Earth's surface on April 13, 2029, closer than many satellites in geosynchronous orbit. NASA says there is no risk of Apophis hitting our planet for at least 100 years. OSIRIS-APEX, the renamed OSIRIS-REx spacecraft, will rendezvous with Apophis in June 2029 to study how Earth's gravity changes its orbit, spin and surface.

Source: NASA, Asteroid Apophis

Source: NASA, OSIRIS-APEX

Knowing how a stony asteroid reacts to a close pass is exactly the kind of knowledge planetary defense needs.

For Further Information: NASA, OSIRIS-APEX

The Five W's of OSIRIS-APEX

Who

NASA, reusing the OSIRIS-REx spacecraft that returned asteroid Bennu samples.

Source: NASA, OSIRIS-APEX

What

An asteroid rendezvous mission studying how a close pass of Earth affects a stony asteroid.

Source: NASA, OSIRIS-APEX

When

Apophis passes Earth April 13, 2029; OSIRIS-APEX rendezvous in June 2029.

Source: NASA, Asteroid Apophis

Source: NASA, OSIRIS-APEX

Where

Asteroid 99942 Apophis, about 20,000 miles from Earth's surface at closest approach.

Source: NASA, Asteroid Apophis

Why

To learn how asteroids respond to gravity and what they are made of, knowledge that informs planetary defense.

Source: NASA, OSIRIS-APEX

OSIRIS-APEX: Mission Status

Cruising to Apophis; rendezvous June 2029. Dates per NASA; missions shift.

Ramses (ESA / JAXA)

Agency solution · active (in development, contracted)

Impact Statement

ESA's Rapid Apophis Mission for Space Safety, Ramses, will rendezvous with Apophis before its rare close encounter with Earth. ESA signed the €81.2 million build contract with OHB Italia on February 10, 2026, and the spacecraft was switched on for the first time in September 2026. It must reach space by spring 2028 on a Japanese H3 rocket. JAXA contributes the solar arrays and a thermal infrared imager.

Source: ESA, Ramses contracts

Source: ESA, First switch-on for Ramses

Building on Hera, Ramses strengthens international cooperation and Europe's planetary defense capabilities.

For Further Information: ESA, Ramses contracts

Image: ESA.

The Five W's of Ramses

Who

ESA and JAXA, with OHB Italia as prime contractor.

Source: ESA, Ramses contracts

What

A spacecraft with two CubeSats that will study Apophis before, during and after its Earth pass.

Source: ESA, Ramses contracts

When

Contract signed February 10, 2026; first switch-on September 2026; launch by spring 2028.

Source: ESA, Ramses contracts

Source: ESA, First switch-on for Ramses

Where

Asteroid Apophis, which passes Earth on Friday, April 13, 2029.

Source: ESA, First switch-on for Ramses

Why

To understand asteroids' physical properties and behaviour and strengthen planetary defense.

Source: ESA, Ramses contracts

Ramses: Mission Status

In development. ESA: launch on an H3 rocket by spring 2028 (no exact date published). Dates per ESA; missions shift.
