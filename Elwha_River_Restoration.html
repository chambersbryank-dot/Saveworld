Elwha River Restoration

Two Elwha River dams came down between 2011 and 2014, and more than 20 million tons of trapped sediment flowed downstream to rebuild the river and coast (NPS, USGS).

Created by SI and Biological SI Partner.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 2 sources cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • The Elwha River had two dams that stopped salmon from swimming home.
  • Between 2011 and 2014 both dams were taken down.
  • Sand and gravel stuck behind the dams washed down the river and helped rebuild the beach, and the river runs free again.

The Five W's of Elwha River Restoration

Who

Olympic National Park with the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe, NOAA, USGS, and fish and wildlife agencies.

Source: National Park Service, Elwha River Restoration

What

The removal of the Elwha and Glines Canyon dams so the river could flow free from the mountains to the sea.

Source: National Park Service, Elwha River Restoration

When

Congress approved it in 1992. Work began September 17, 2011, and the last dam was gone in 2014.

Source: National Park Service, Elwha River Restoration

Where

The Elwha River on the Olympic Peninsula in Washington State.

Source: National Park Service, Elwha River Restoration

Why

The dams blocked salmon, trapped Sand, mud and gravel carried by water that settles on river beds, lake bottoms and beaches. Glossary and flooded homelands of the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe.

Source: National Park Service, Elwha River Restoration

Impact Statement

Agency solution · active

More than 20 million tons of sediment that had piled up behind the dams moved downstream in the first five years, rebuilding the river bed and the beach at the river mouth.

Source: USGS, Moving Mountains: Elwha River still changing

For Further Information: National Park Service, Elwha River Restoration

After two dams came out, more than 20 million tons of sand and gravel washed down the Elwha River to rebuild its beaches.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

The salmon runs were an important food source for the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe; the restored river reconnects the Tribe with its homelands and fish.

Source: National Park Service, Elwha River Restoration

For Nature

The Elwha once hosted eleven kinds of sea-run salmon and trout, and today it flows freely again from the Olympic Mountains to the Strait of Juan de Fuca.

Source: National Park Service, Elwha River Restoration
