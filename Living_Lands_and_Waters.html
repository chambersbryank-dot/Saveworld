Living Lands & Waters

A barge-based river cleanup crew that, with more than 141,000 volunteers, has pulled over 14 million pounds of trash from U.S. rivers (organization figure).

Image: NASA/JPL (PIA23534, Mississippi River).

  1. Home
  2. Rivers

Last reviewed: October 2026 · 1 source cited

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • Living Lands & Waters is a team that lives on a big boat called a barge.
  • They travel down rivers and pick up trash with help from volunteers.
  • They have picked up more than 14 million pounds of trash and planted millions of trees.

The Five W's of Living Lands & Waters

Who

Living Lands & Waters, a nonprofit founded in 1998 and based in East Moline, Illinois.

Source: Living Lands & Waters, Mission and History

What

River cleanups, tree plantings and workshops run from a working barge, with volunteers doing the hauling.

Source: Living Lands & Waters, Mission and History

When

Since 1998; the crew lives and travels on the barge up to nine months a year.

Source: Living Lands & Waters, Mission and History

Where

America's major rivers, starting with the Mississippi River and its All the land where rain and snow drain into the same river or lake. Glossary.

Source: Living Lands & Waters, Mission and History

Why

Trash dumped or washed into rivers harms wildlife and the water that towns use.

Source: Living Lands & Waters, Mission and History

Impact Statement

Nonprofit solution · active

Living Lands & Waters reports 14,233,270 pounds of trash removed, 141,360 volunteers engaged and 2,496,900 trees planted (organization figures).

Source: Living Lands & Waters, Mission and History

For Further Information: Living Lands & Waters, Mission and History

Living Lands & Waters volunteers have pulled more than 14 million pounds of trash out of U.S. rivers.

How This Saves People & the Planet

For People

Cleanups give volunteers a hands-on way to care for their own river, and the group has taught more than 31,000 students (organization figure).

Source: Living Lands & Waters, Mission and History

For Nature

Removing tires, barrels and plastic from riverbanks keeps it out of fish habitat, and millions of planted trees help hold riverbanks together.

Source: Living Lands & Waters, Mission and History
