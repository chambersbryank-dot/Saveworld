Phytoplankton, the Ocean's Oxygen Engine

Tiny ocean plants produce about half of Earth's oxygen, making phytoplankton one of the planet's most important natural systems.

Image: NASA.

💡 Explain Like I'm 10

  • What is it? A natural system that turns sunlight and A gas that people and animals breathe out and that burning fuel releases. Plants take it in, and too much of it in the air warms the planet. Glossary into food and oxygen.
  • Why does it matter? Healthy oceans mean breathable air and a working food web.

The Five W's of Phytoplankton

Who

Tiny plant-like living things that drift in water and use sunlight to make food. They make much of the oxygen we breathe and feed ocean life. Glossary, drifting plants, algae and bacteria that photosynthesize near the ocean surface.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

What

A natural system that turns sunlight and carbon dioxide into food and oxygen.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

When

Every day; marine life uses about as much oxygen as phytoplankton produce.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

Where

The sunlit upper layer of every ocean; satellites like NASA's PACE track it from space.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

Source: NASA, PACE mission

Why

Healthy oceans mean breathable air and a working food web.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?

Impact Statement

Natural solution

About half of Earth's oxygen comes from the ocean, and most of it is made by phytoplankton, drifting plants and algae too small to see one by one. A single kind, Prochlorococcus, produces up to 20% of the oxygen in our whole biosphere. Keeping the ocean healthy keeps this oxygen engine running.

Source: NOAA, How much oxygen comes from the ocean?
